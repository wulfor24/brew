WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This discipline applies to Arata directly. Where the playbook says "signup", read account creation and WhatsApp or email opt-in. Where it says
"subscription", read the 45-day reorder. Where it says "onboarding", read the first 84 days of using the serum.
Arata does about ₹61 Cr a year, 50,000+ D2C orders a month and ~650K monthly web visits. Only 32% of that revenue runs through arata.in, the
one surface where the brand can see what a customer did before buying. The other 68% sits on Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and
Instamart, where the brand gets order counts and a seller dashboard but no funnel. So when the ₹160 Cr plan asks "which concern, which SKU,
which creative and which channel should get the next rupee", the honest answer today is: we can see a third of the picture.
The website side is probably measured the way most Shopify stores are measured (assumption, since we have not seen the tag container): the
Google & YouTube app sending default GA4 ecommerce, a Meta pixel from the Shopify Meta app, Loox for reviews, and maybe an older GTM
container from before Shopify locked the checkout. That setup gives sessions, add-to-carts and purchases. It does not tell you whether a visitor
came in for hair fall or dandruff, whether they opened the 7.4X study, whether they looked at MitoActive and then bought the ₹571 Intensive
Serum instead, or whether a buyer came back inside 45 days.
That is expensive. The Samay Raina mock podcast was judged on recall, because recall was the only thing anyone could read. MitoActive went
live at ₹1,499 with zero reviews and nobody can say what share of PDP viewers bounce to the cheaper serum. The 45-day repeat, the number
that decides whether Arata behaves like a 26% beauty brand or a 40%+ routine brand, is not a number anyone looks at weekly. And with COD
orders that cancel or return, the "revenue" GA4 reports is gross, so the Meta and Google algorithms are being fed inflated purchase values.
The opportunity is to build the measurement layer once, properly, before the rebrand, the quiz, subscription and the MitoActive push all land on
top of it. Every one of those initiatives needs the same four things: a clean data layer, a concern-level view of the catalogue, a new-vs-repeat
split, and one weekly page that puts all three channels side by side. We build those four things in 30 days. Everything else the deployment team
ships for Arata reads its score off this.

SHOPIFY CONSTRAINT WE HAVE TO DESIGN AROUND
Shopify removed script access to checkout in 2024. GTM in the theme can no longer see checkout steps or the thank-you page. Any tracking
plan for Arata that puts begin_checkout, add_payment_info and purchase inside a theme GTM container will fail silently. The correct pattern:
theme GTM handles storefront events; a Shopify Customer Events custom pixel handles checkout and purchase; both push to the same GA4

stores at Arata's size have purchase firing twice (app plus pixel) and nobody notices until ROAS looks 1.6X better in GA4 than in Shopify. We
check for this on day 1.

WHAT THE CATALOGUE LOOKS LIKE TO ANALYTICS TODAY
Arata's catalogue has a clear concern structure on the site (Hair Growth, Anti-Dandruff, Damage Defence, Anti-Grey, Styling, Body, Face, Tools).

THE PLAN
Three phases. Foundation first, Arata-specific events second, the channel layer third. Each phase ships something leadership can read.

PHASE 1 (DAYS 1 TO 10): FOUNDATION AND HYGIENE
What: New GTM container, GA4 property audit, Shopify Customer Events pixel for checkout, consent defaults, internal traffic filter, purchase
deduplication, 14-month retention, Google Ads and Merchant Center links.
Why: Nothing built on top is trustworthy until purchase fires once with the right value and items, and until checkout steps exist.
Expected effect: Purchase count in GA4 within 3% of Shopify orders for the same day. begin_checkout and add_payment_info populated. One
source of truth for Meta and Google bidding signals.
Decisions we make in this phase:
   GA4 purchase counting method: every event. Value: order subtotal after discounts, before shipping, in INR.
   COD orders fire purchase at order placement (same as prepaid) with payment_type: cod . Cancellations and RTO come back as refund events
   via a Shopify webhook to the GA4 Measurement Protocol in Phase 2. Until then, the scorecard shows gross web revenue with a COD share
   column so nobody reads it as net.
   Consent: Consent Mode v2 with defaults set to denied for ad_storage until the visitor interacts with a single-line notice; analytics_storage
   granted by default with IP anonymisation. This is the pragmatic reading of the DPDP Act for a cosmetics store with no EU traffic. Arata's legal
   counsel confirms the default before go-live.
   Google Signals on, so cross-device audiences can export to Google Ads.

PHASE 2 (DAYS 8 TO 20): ARATA-SPECIFIC EVENTS AND PROPERTIES
What: The 31-event tracking plan in Worked example 1, the concern vocabulary, user properties for customer type and order count, marketplace
link clicks, claim proof views, routine views, opt-ins, review submission, refund sync.
Why: This is where the eight decisions become readable. Concern-level CVR, MitoActive cross-shopping, claim lift and 45-day cohorts all come
from these events.
Expected effect: Within two weeks of data, the growth team can split every Meta campaign by concern and by new vs repeat, and can see the
MitoActive → Intensive Serum downgrade rate.
The event design follows one rule: GA4 recommended names stay as they are (view_item, add_to_cart, begin_checkout, purchase, refund,
sign_up, search, select_promotion). Everything Arata-specific uses object_action in lowercase with context in parameters, never in the event
name. So claim_proof_viewed with claim_value: 7.4x , not claim_7x_viewed .

PHASE 3 (DAYS 15 TO 30): CHANNEL LAYER AND THE WEEKLY SCORECARD
What: GA4 BigQuery export on; weekly CSV imports from Amazon Seller Central, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart into the same
BigQuery project; one Looker Studio scorecard refreshed Monday 9am IST; UTM standard enforced through a shared link builder sheet.

UTM STANDARD FOR ARATA
Lowercase, underscores, no spaces, no brand name in campaign (it is always Arata). Five parameters, all of them documented in one sheet that
doubles as the link builder.

DATA QUALITY RULES
    Purchase fires once. The Google & YouTube app GA4 toggle is off. The custom pixel sends purchase with transaction_id = Shopify order
    name. GTM has a trigger exception if the same transaction_id was seen this session.
    Internal traffic: Delhi HQ and warehouse IPs, plus any session with ?qa=1 , filtered from the reporting view.
    Test orders tagged test_order: true in the data layer and excluded.
    Value is read from the Shopify checkout object in major units (rupees), never parsed from rendered price text.
    Every Monday the scorecard shows GA4 purchases vs Shopify orders for the prior week. Drift above 3% opens a ticket.

     });

   analytics.subscribe('checkout_completed', function (e) {
     var c = e.data.checkout;
