WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Arata sells on six shelves: arata.in, Amazon.in, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart. On five of
them the image is the store. There is no sales page, no Lab Notes explainer, no Samay Raina podcast.
There is a 1:1 thumbnail, maybe 150 pixels wide on a phone, next to Pilgrim, Minimalist and Mamaearth.
68% of Arata's revenue is decided in that square.
The brand's single best asset is the number on the bottle: 7.4X, 28,200, 95%, 86%, 87%. Today that
number lives on the pack and in the product page copy. On a Blinkit tile it is unreadable. On Amazon
the secondary image slots, the A+ modules and the Brand Story carousel are where that number
should do its work, and they are the slots most Indian D2C brands fill last and worst. We have not
audited Arata's live listings image by image (that is week 1), but the brief tells us enough: naming is
inconsistent across channels, the legacy curl-care and onion-biotin lines are still visible on
marketplaces, and the rebrand to "Hair & Scalp Science Co." is mid-flight. That means at least two
visual languages are live at once. A customer who sees the lab-blue 3.0 serum on arata.in and a 2022
Super Shampoo on Nykaa does not see one brand.
Two product facts make images urgent. MitoActive launched in August at ₹1,499 with zero reviews.
With no social proof, the image stack has to carry the whole argument: what Y100 is, why
mitochondria matter, what 87% in 84 days means, and why ₹1,499 is ₹50 a day. The second fact is the
complaint theme in the reviews: "the seal was broken", "the shampoo bottle was leaking", "it doesn't
have the box". Those are operations problems, but a shot of the sealed cap and the carton in slot 4 of
the listing answers the anxiety before it becomes a one-star review.
The cost of leaving this alone is quiet. Marketplace conversion a point or two below what the claim
deserves. Meta statics rebuilt from scratch per campaign. Rebrand assets that reach arata.in and
never reach Zepto. Lab Notes posts shared on WhatsApp with no preview image, which reads as
broken.

WHICH IMAGES MAY BE GENERATED AND WHICH MAY NOT
The decision that matters most for a regulated haircare brand is where image generation is allowed.
Arata carries numeric claims on every pack. An image model will redraw a label, invent a percentage,
add a leaf to a bottle that says "good science", or render a seal that does not match the real one. For a
brand repositioning from "zero chemicals" to bioscience, a hallucinated bottle is a credibility problem
and potentially an ASCI complaint.
                                                                                                                              DIAGRAM
    Is the bottle, label, cap, carton or claim text in the frame?

    └── No ↓
    Is there a number, a claim, or the brand name in the image?

THE PLAN
PHASE 1 (WEEK 1): AUDIT AND MASTER LIBRARY
What: Screenshot every live listing image on Amazon.in, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit and Zepto for the ten
hero SKUs. Score each on four checks: readable at 150px, claim visible, matches 3.0 visual language,
shows seal or carton. Then shoot the master library in one studio day in Delhi: MitoActive, Hair Growth
Intensive Serum, Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo, Anti-Breakage Conditioner, Anti-Dandruff Shampoo, Anti-
Dandruff Conditioner, Anti-Dandruff Serum, Damage Defence Super Shampoo, Anti-Grey Serum, Hair
Gel. Five angles each (front, three-quarter, back label, cap and seal close-up, carton), plus the Hair
Growth Trio and Anti-Dandruff S+C as grouped shots, plus hands, dropper and scalp-application
sequences.
Why: Every other asset references this library. Without a real packshot the generated scenes will drift.
Expected effect: A single source of truth that ends the onion-vs-biotin, serum-vs-tonic drift across
channels. The legacy curl-care and Super Shampoo images stay as they are until Arata decides
whether those SKUs survive the rebrand; we do not reshoot what might be retired.

PHASE 2 (WEEKS 1 TO 2): THE 7-SLOT STACK
What: One template, seven slots, every SKU. The order is fixed because it mirrors how a shopper
scrolls a marketplace gallery.
                                                                                                             DIAGRAM
    Slot 1    MAIN        Packshot on pure white. 85% fill. No text. (Amazon, Google Shopping rule)
    Slot 2    THE NUMBER Claim card. One number, one unit, one footnote. "7.4X hair fall reduction*"
    Slot 3    HOW IT WORKS Mechanism macro + 3 labelled actives. Y100 / Hairdian AP / rosemary-caffeine
    Slot 4    IN HAND     Real hands, dropper or pump, scale of the bottle. Answers "is 30 ml enough?"
    Slot 5    THE ROUTINE The SKU in its system: shampoo → serum → conditioner, with the 84-day marker
    Slot 6    SEAL + BOX Cap, tamper seal, carton. Answers the leak and "no box" reviews
    Slot 7    REAL PEOPLE Verbatim review on a card, star count, platform. "Hair fall reduced drastically"

PHASE 3 (WEEK 2): QUICK-COMMERCE TILES
What: A tile variant of slot 1 for Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart. Same packshot, cropped tighter (product
fills 92% of frame), with the concern word set large in the brand colour band at the bottom where the
platform allows a non-white background: "HAIR FALL", "DANDRUFF", "GROWTH". Where the platform
requires pure white, the pack's own front-label number has to be legible, so we supply a packshot
shot straight on with the label face lit evenly.
Why: Quick commerce is 30% of revenue and the tile is the only creative. At 150px, "Arata Anti-Hair
Fall Intensive Shampoo 200 ml" is unreadable; "HAIR FALL" and a blue bottle are not.
Expected effect: Recognition on the shelf against Mamaearth and Dove, which is the actual fight on
Blinkit.

PHASE 4 (WEEKS 2 TO 3): AMAZON A+ AND BRAND STORY
What: A+ for the three hair-fall SKUs and the Anti-Dandruff Shampoo first, in this module order:
header image with text (the routine), four-image and text (the actives), comparison chart (the Arata
hair growth ladder: Intensive Serum ₹571 → Pack of 3 ₹999 → MitoActive ₹1,499), single image and
dark text overlay (seal and lab). Then a Brand Story carousel that carries the rebrand: the Delhi lab,
"systems, not symptoms", the Y100 licence, the founders.
Why: Amazon is the largest single channel inside the 38% marketplace share. A+ is free real estate
below the fold that Pilgrim and Minimalist already use well. The comparison chart is the only place on
Amazon where Arata can upsell its own ladder on a competitor's page view.
Expected effect: Cross-sell inside Amazon without ad spend; a Brand Story that stops the Super
Shampoo listing from looking like a different company.

PHASE 5 (WEEK 3): META STATIC SYSTEM
What: Twelve statics built from the format map in Arata's ad creative plan, using the library: three
claim statements, three "us vs them" (Traya monthly kit vs one serum; onion shampoo vs a number;
minoxidil dependency vs a 3-month course), three review cards, three FAQ cards (₹1,499, "another
Redensyl serum?", "do I use it forever?"). Backgrounds generated, type in Figma, exported 1080x1080,
1080x1350 and 1080x1920.
Why: Statics are the cheapest creative to iterate and the easiest to keep on-brand when the type lives
in a template.
Expected effect: A reusable static set that the ad account can refresh weekly by swapping a
background or a verbatim rather than rebuilding.

a conversion lever on mobile data in tier-2 cities. The OG template makes every WhatsApp share of a
Lab Notes post look like Arata.
Expected effect: Faster PDPs, measurable on Core Web Vitals; shared links that carry the number.
