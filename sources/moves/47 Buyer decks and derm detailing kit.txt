WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Sales enablement in software means decks, one-pagers and objection docs for account executives.
For Arata it means the same assets pointed at four B2B gatekeepers: quick-commerce category
managers, marketplace account managers, dermatologists and trichologists, and salon owners. The
consumer sees a product page. These four people see a sales kit, or nothing.
Today they mostly see nothing. Arata moves roughly 2 lakh B2B units a month into marketplace and
quick-commerce warehouses, and 68% of FY25 revenue came through those channels (marketplaces
38%, quick commerce 30%). Every rupee of that passes through a human who decides shelf position,
search placement, which SKU gets the deal banner and whether the brand gets a slot in the Diwali or
monsoon promo calendar. That person is not persuaded by "87% saw increased growth in 84 days".
They are persuaded by units per dark store per day, fill rate, return rate and co-funded promo money.
Arata has the first set of numbers on every bottle and the second set in a spreadsheet nobody has
turned into a page.
The second gap is MitoActive. It launched in August 2026 at ₹1,499 with zero reviews. The persona
who buys it, Clinical Kavya, has already seen a dermatologist and is price-insensitive. She does not
need a Reel. She needs her derm to say the name. Y100 has 6+ years of research and 5+ clinical
studies, including an Indian-participant study. That is a detailing story. Nobody is detailing it.
The third gap is consistency. Naming differs across channels (onion vs biotin, serum vs tonic), the
legacy Curl Care range still sits on marketplaces, and the "seal was broken" and "bottle was leaking"
reviews show up on exactly the listings a category manager checks before a range review. A buyer
who sees a muddled catalogue and a 1-star leak review takes the facing away from Arata and gives it
to Pilgrim.
What this costs: slower dark-store expansion beyond the metros, a ₹1,499 hero serum with no clinical
channel behind it, and a ₹160 Cr target that leans on channels where Arata has no sales collateral.
What it earns if fixed: a repeatable way to win range reviews, a derm channel that seeds MitoActive
reviews, and a salon channel for the styling range that is already Arata's most-reviewed line on Loox.

the 3.0 range.

KIT 3: DERMATOLOGIST DETAILING KIT FOR MITOACTIVE (WEEKS 2 TO 4)
What: a two-sided detailing aid, a one-page study summary, a 4-minute detail script, a sample kit
(assumption: 5 ml trial size, to be produced) and a patient leaflet with an 84-day check-in card. Why:
MitoActive has zero reviews and a ₹1,499 price. The derm is the only person who can say "try this
instead of minoxidil" with authority. Each derm who recommends it seeds a review from a patient who
was going to pay anyway. Expected effect: the first 100 verified MitoActive reviews come from derm-
referred patients, and "dermatologist recommended" becomes a true line on the listing.

KIT 4: SALON TRADE KIT (WEEKS 3 TO 4)
What: a trade price list, a counter card for the retail shelf, a stylist script and a client reorder QR to
arata.in. Why: styling combos are already Arata's most-reviewed line. Salons are the sampling channel
for styling. Men who buy gel at a salon are the Styling Sahil persona at the moment of trust. Expected
effect: a pilot of 25 salons in Delhi and Bangalore (assumption on cities, chosen for HQ proximity and
the Hair-fall Rahul persona) with a reorder path that lands on arata.in rather than on rented land.
