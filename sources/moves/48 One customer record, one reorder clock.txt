WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In software, revenue operations means the plumbing between marketing, sales and customer success:
who owns a lead, when it moves stage, how fast someone follows up. For Arata the same plumbing
connects four things: the Shopify store, the marketplaces, the quick-commerce platforms and the
small B2B motion. The lead is a customer. The handoff is from first order to second order. The sales
rep is a WhatsApp message, a support agent, or a category manager relationship. Same discipline,
different nouns.
Arata today runs three revenue systems that do not talk to each other. The website is 32% of revenue
and produces a customer record with a phone number. Marketplaces are 38% and produce an order
with no usable identity. Quick commerce is 30% and produces a purchase order and a fill rate. Two
lakh B2B units a month flow through POs with no pipeline behind them. The stated key conversion
action in the brand brief is "first purchase, then repeat within 45 days." There is no system that knows
which customer is on Day 30, which SKU they bought, or whether they have a leaking bottle sitting in a
complaint queue.
That gap has a price. The brief puts beauty repeat rates around 26% and routine-led models above
40%. Arata sells 84-day courses and calls itself "systems, not symptoms", but with no subscription,
no quiz, no reorder clock and no single customer record, it is operating like a 26% brand. On 50,000
D2C orders a month at an implied AOV of ₹900 to ₹1,000, every one-point lift in 45-day repeat rate is
roughly 500 extra orders a month, or about ₹4.75 lakh in revenue (assumption: ₹950 AOV, repeat
measured on the monthly cohort). Ten points is ₹5.7 Cr a year from customers Arata already paid to
acquire.
Two smaller leaks compound it. Leak and broken-seal complaints are a known review theme ("the seal
was broken", "the shampoo bottle was leaking"). A complaint with no SLA and no link to the customer
record means a Day 30 reorder nudge can land on someone who is still waiting for a replacement. And

DATA HYGIENE: IDENTITY AND SKU NAMING
Identity. Shopify stores phone and email per customer, but the same person can exist as three
records: a guest checkout with phone, an account with email, and a WhatsApp contact from a
campaign reply. Dedup today is manual or absent (assumption). The rule we set: phone in E.164 format
is the primary key; email is the secondary key; a match on either merges, with the most recent address
and the earliest first-order date surviving.
SKU naming. The brief notes "onion vs biotin, serum vs tonic" and the Super Shampoo listed on Nykaa
at 300 ml / ₹899 against the 200 ml site range. A reorder clock needs one SKU ID per product per size
and one days-of-supply value per SKU. Today that table does not exist. The appendix carries the
master SKU map we build in week 1.
Attribution. UTM discipline is unknown (assumption: campaign-level only). For revenue operations we
need one field on every order: acquisition_channel with a closed list (site_paid, site_organic,
site_creator, site_whatsapp, amazon, nykaa, flipkart, blinkit, zepto, instamart, b2b_derm, b2b_salon,
b2b_corporate, b2b_distributor). The attribution document in this series handles the model; this
document handles the field.

STACK ASSUMPTIONS
Shopify is confirmed. Everything else is an assumption we will verify in week 1: a WhatsApp Business
API provider (Interakt, Wati, Zoko or similar), an email tool (Klaviyo or similar), an order management
layer for marketplace orders (Unicommerce or EasyEcom or similar), and no CRM for B2B. We do not
recommend replacing any of it. We recommend making Shopify Customers the consumer system of
record, a lightweight CRM (HubSpot Starter or Zoho CRM, both priced for Indian teams) the B2B
system of record, and a warehouse table in the OMS or a sheet the channel order ledger for
marketplace and quick-commerce aggregates.
WhatsApp cost is small enough to treat as free at this scale. Marketing messages in India cost roughly
₹0.78 per message and utility messages roughly ₹0.12 (assumption: Meta's per-message pricing
effective July 2025; confirm current rate with the provider). A Day 30 nudge to a month's 50,000
customers is about ₹39,000.



THE PLAN
The plan has three layers. Define the record and the stages. Automate the handoffs. Open the B2B
pipeline. In that order, because automating an undefined stage produces broken results faster.

LAYER 1: ONE CUSTOMER RECORD AND THE PACK-TO-PROFILE BRIDGE
What. Shopify Customers becomes the system of record for every consumer. Every other tool writes
to it or reads from it. We add six custom fields: lifecycle_stage , primary_concern (hair fall, dandruff,
damage, grey, styling, body, face), last_sku_id , last_sku_days_of_supply , reorder_due_date ,
 complaint_open (true/false). The WhatsApp provider and email tool sync stage and reorder date back
to the record daily.



BREW DEPLOYMENT TEAM × ARATA · ONE CUSTOMER RECORD, ONE REORDER CLOCK                                     4
The bridge. Every pack and every in-box card carries a QR that opens WhatsApp with a prefilled
message: "Hi Arata, I bought [SKU] on [channel]". The customer sends it, we capture phone, SKU and
channel, and start their reorder clock even though they bought on Blinkit. The reward for sending it is
the replacement promise ("if the bottle leaked or the seal was broken, reply LEAK and a new one ships
in 48 hours") plus the 84-day routine guide. This is the only way to get identity from 68% of revenue.
Why. Without one record there is no stage, no clock, no suppression and no repeat metric. Without
the bridge, the reorder system only works on a third of the business.
Expected effect. Identity capture on marketplace and quick-commerce orders goes from zero to a
measurable number (target 8% of units in 90 days, assumption based on scan rates for packaging QR
with a clear reward).

LAYER 3: REORDER-READINESS SCORE (THE MQL RULE)
Fit is what they bought. Engagement is what they did since. Negative signals block. A score of 60 or
more routes to a human WhatsApp conversation from CX. 35 to 59 gets the automated nudge
sequence. Under 35 gets nothing until the clock advances. Any open complaint overrides everything.
The full specification with three scored customers is worked example B.
Why. A ₹1,499 MitoActive customer on Day 30 who has visited the MitoActive page twice deserves a
person, not a template. A ₹269 styling cream buyer does not need a nudge at all. Scoring tells the
team where the human minutes go.
