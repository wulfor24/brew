WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In SaaS, onboarding runs from signup to the aha moment. For Arata, signup is the first order delivered,
the aha moment is the day the customer sees less hair in the drain, and retention is the second and
third bottle. Activation is the thing in between: did they use it, correctly, every day, for long enough to
notice. That is what this document builds.
Arata sells a result that takes 84 days and ships it in a 30-day bottle. The Hair Growth Intensive Serum
says "up to +28,200 new hair within 84 days". MitoActive says "87% saw increased growth in 84
days". The Anti-Grey Serum is a 3-month plan. The claim on the pack is a promise about day 84. The
bottle runs out on day 30. Nothing on arata.in today bridges that gap: no routine builder, no quiz, no
subscription, no reminder, no check-in, no community. The customer opens the box, reads a label,
guesses the dose, and is on her own.
The reviews tell you the aha moment is real and reachable. "I can see a visible difference in two
weeks." "Hair fall reduced drastically." That is the moment to engineer the whole first month around.
The reviews also tell you where the path breaks before it starts: "the seal was broken", "the shampoo
bottle was leaking", "it doesn't have the box". A leaking bottle on day 0 is a customer who paid and
never got to step one.
The cost is in the repeat rate. Arata does 50,000+ D2C orders a month at an implied AOV of ₹900 to
₹1,000. Beauty repeat rates sit near 26%. Routine-led models exceed 40%. What separates those two
numbers is whether the customer used the product long enough to believe it. Trial-abandonment data
from software says 40 to 60% of new users quit after a single session and most of that happens in
day one. Our assumption for a hair serum is the same shape: the biggest leak is customers who apply
it three or four times, see nothing, and leave the bottle in the cabinet. The product never got its 14
days.
