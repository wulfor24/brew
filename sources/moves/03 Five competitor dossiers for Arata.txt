WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In SaaS this discipline means scraping rival pricing pages and pulling domain-authority data. For Arata
it means profiling the five brands that sit next to it on a Blinkit search, a Nykaa listing, an Amazon.in
"customers also viewed" row and a Meta feed: Traya, Pilgrim, Minimalist, Bare Anatomy and
Mamaearth. The pages we read are product pages and hair-test funnels. The "reviews" are Nykaa and
Amazon ratings. The method is the same. Facts first, inference labelled, implications framed as
choices.
Today Arata's view of its competitors is a paragraph. The brand brief carries one line per rival: Pilgrim
has "no clinical number", Minimalist is a "cold brand", Traya is "expensive, slow, medicalised". Those
lines are probably right. None of them is dated, sourced or specific enough to put in an ad. The
objection script for "₹1,499 for 30 ml is a lot" says to compare MitoActive to Traya at ₹1,400 to ₹1,800
a month. Traya's own cost page, read this week, puts the six-month plan at ₹1,400 a month, which is
₹47 a day. MitoActive at ₹1,499 for a 30-day bottle is ₹50 a day. The comparison Arata planned to
make on price is a comparison it loses by ₹3. The comparison it wins is on commitment: one bottle
versus a five-month lock-in with minoxidil, supplements and a refund that only pays out if you never
miss a month. That is the kind of detail a dossier catches and a paragraph does not.
The cost of running without profiles shows up in three places. The planned "Arata vs Traya" and
"Arata vs Pilgrim" pages will quote prices that drift within a quarter. The MitoActive launch, which
already has zero reviews, will be argued against the wrong rival (Pilgrim's ₹699 serum) when the
customer paying ₹1,499 is actually weighing Traya and minoxidil. And the quick-commerce category
pitch, where 30% of revenue lives, needs a clean answer to "why you and not Mamaearth on the
shelf" that is grounded in what Mamaearth's listing says today.
The opportunity is bigger than defence. Arata is the only brand in the ₹350 to ₹700 band that prints a
study number on every bottle. Minimalist prints a percentage of actives. Pilgrim prints an ingredient
list. Bare Anatomy prints a quiz result. Mamaearth prints "goodness". Traya prints a number, but

KEY TAKEAWAYS
1. MitoActive's real competitor is Traya, and the honest comparison is on commitment and
   composition. On price, per day the two are within ₹3 of each other at Traya's cheapest tier.
2. The Intensive Serum at ₹571 is the cheapest hair-growth serum among the actives-led brands and
   the only one with an outcome number. That is a lead Arata is under-using while the pack-of-3 at
   ₹999 trains customers to wait for 49% off.
3. Two competitor mechanics are missing at Arata and sit on its own known-gaps list: a quiz (Bare
   Anatomy, Traya) and a monthly cadence (Traya). Both are funnel features, and both are buildable
   without a new formula.
4. Mamaearth is the brand Arata's customers come from. The right posture is trade-up, and the right
   venue is the quick-commerce shelf.
5. Every "not observed" in the comparison table stays a question until it is checked on the
   competitor's own page. Only then does it become a line Arata can use.

WHAT THE PROFILES CHANGE AT ARATA
Each recommendation names the profile it comes from, what to do, and the effect we expect.
From Traya: reframe the MitoActive objection script. Replace "compare to Traya at ₹1,400 to ₹1,800
a month" with a commitment comparison: one ₹1,499 bottle, no doctor, no minoxidil, no five-month

From Traya: price MitoActive as a course. Traya's table rewards the six-month commitment with the

From Pilgrim, Minimalist, Bare Anatomy: one line for the ₹571 serum. The three brands disclose

style) to a named routine with one price and the study number for that routine. Expected effect: higher
