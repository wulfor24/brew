WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This is a D2C read of CRO. The pages are product pages, collection pages and the cart, and the
conversion is a first order of a hair fall or dandruff routine on arata.in, then a reorder inside 45 days.
Arata gets about 650K visits a month and ships 50,000+ D2C orders a month. On paper that is 7.7
visits per order. That number includes repeat buyers coming straight to the cart, so the real first-visit
conversion rate is lower and nobody has it instrumented yet. Our working assumption, based on
Shopify beauty benchmarks and the catalogue's price band, is a new-visitor rate between 2% and
3%. That is already above the Shopify median, which is roughly 1.4%. It is also well short of what a
brand with a number on every bottle should get.
The maths on the gap is simple. Half a point of conversion on 650K visits is 3,250 extra orders a
month. At the implied ₹900 to ₹1,000 AOV that is close to ₹31 lakh a month, or around ₹3.7 Cr a year,
from the same traffic and the same ad spend. Traffic is growing about 42% month on month, so every
point we fix now compounds.
The site has a specific set of leaks and the brief names them. The hair growth collection mixes a
₹1,499 serum, a ₹571 serum, a ₹359 shampoo and a ₹999 pack of 3 with no routine builder, so a visitor
with hair fall has to decide between four claims on their own. MitoActive is the hero of the rebrand and
has zero reviews, which on a ₹1,499 product is a conversion wall. Leak and broken-seal complaints
are the top negative review theme and no page answers them. Naming is inconsistent across channels
(onion vs biotin, serum vs tonic), so a visitor who saw the product on Nykaa may not recognise it on
arata.in. There is no quiz, no subscription, and no "which one is for me" path.
The opportunity is that Arata already has the hardest CRO asset to build: proof. 7.4X, 28,200, 95%,
86%, 87% in 84 days, 2,248 on-site reviews, a Nykaa 4.2★ on 2,435 ratings, Unilever Ventures and
L'Oréal BOLD on the cap table, an in-house lab. Most of it is scattered or buried. The work is
arrangement and sequencing, not invention. We put the right number next to the right button for the
right persona, remove the four or five friction points that cost orders every day, and test the rest.

DIMENSION-BY-DIMENSION FINDINGS
1. Value proposition clarity. The brand line "Good science for great hair" and "Hair & Scalp Science
Co." describe the company. They do not tell Hair-fall Rahul in 5 seconds what happens to his hair. The
strongest first-visit line Arata owns is already in the catalogue: "Up to 28,200 new hair in 84 days."
The homepage should lead with the outcome, the timeline and the price, in that order. The MitoActive
line "India's first mitochondrial hair growth treatment" is a category claim. It needs the 87% in 84 days
number beside it or it reads as jargon.
2. Headline effectiveness. Single PDPs are good because the claim is the headline. The collection
page is weak because it has no headline that sorts the visitor. "Hair Growth" is a category label. A
sorting headline reads: "Hair fall? Start here. Pick your stage, we pick your routine."
3. CTA hierarchy. Every PDP has one job: add to cart. Today the combos (Shampoo + Growth Serum
₹838, Hair Growth Trio ₹1,201) live as separate products, so the upsell happens before the decision
instead of after it. Assumption: there is no sticky add-to-cart on mobile. For a site where most traffic is
mobile and creator-driven, that alone is worth a test.
4. Scannability. The claims are the scan path. "7.4X", "28,200", "95%", "86%" should appear as a
four-tile proof strip on the homepage and the collection, each tile linking to its SKU. Today the
numbers appear one per PDP, so a scanning visitor never sees the pattern that makes Arata different:
a number on every bottle.
