WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
One line on translation first. In D2C the content playbook maps like this: template libraries become
routine pages (the shampoo plus serum plus conditioner cart), lead generation becomes first
purchase and WhatsApp or email opt-in, and case studies become 84-day hair journeys with a real
face and a dated photo.
Arata has the raw material most haircare brands would pay for. A number on every bottle: 7.4X,
28,200, 95%, 86%, 87%. An in-house lab in Delhi. A new mechanism in MitoActive and Y100 that
nobody else in India can sell. Verbatim customers saying "it actually works and it is affordable". That is
a content library waiting to be written. Today almost none of it exists as a page you can find on
Google, send to a friend, or read before you decide to spend ₹1,499.
Here is what the brief shows. Lab Notes posts cluster in bursts (seven in March 2026, then quiet). The
hair growth collection mixes claims with no routine builder. MitoActive launched in August 2026 with
zero reviews. Naming is inconsistent across channels. And 68% of revenue sits on Amazon, Nykaa,
Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart, where Arata rents the shelf and never meets the customer. Content is the
cheapest way to pull that customer onto arata.in, which already does 32% of sales and ~650K visits a
month.
The cost of the gap is specific. The "28,200 hairs? Sounds too good" objection in the brief has no
page answering it, so the claim does the opposite of what it should: it raises suspicion instead of trust.
"Is this just another Redensyl serum?" has no page. "Do I have to use it forever?" has no page.
Meanwhile Traya has built a hair-health blog with dermatologist bylines and a whole "hair stories"
series about people whose hair fall started when they moved to Bengaluru. They are writing the
Indian-hair-and-water story that belongs to Arata, and pointing every reader at a ₹1,400-a-month hair
test.
The opportunity is to own three things in search and in the feed. First, hair fall for Indian hair, Indian
water and Indian weather, which nobody in the ₹400 to ₹700 band owns. Second, "the number,
explained": the only brand that can show the study behind the claim. Third, 84 days as a public clock.

Outline and key sections, written:
Hard water and hair fall: what Delhi, Bengaluru and Chennai water does to your scalp
If your hair fall started within three months of moving cities, read this first. We hear it in reviews and
support messages every week: "shifted to Bangalore for work, hair fall started", "Gurgaon flat,
borewell supply, hair feels like straw". It is one of the five entry points we see most, and it is the one
with the clearest fix.
What hard water does
Hard water carries calcium and magnesium. On hair those minerals leave a film that makes strands
rough, stops conditioner working, and makes the hair snap when you comb. On the scalp they
combine with shampoo to leave a residue that clogs follicles and dries skin, which shows up as itch
and flakes. Most of what people call "hard water hair fall" is breakage, and breakage you can fix in a
few washes.

What 28,200 new hairs actually means
The front of our Hair Growth Intensive Serum says "up to +28,200 new hair within 84 days". We know
how that reads. It reads like a number someone made up. So here is where it comes from, what it does
and does not promise, and why we chose to print it anyway.
Where the number comes from
The claim is from a consumer study on the serum over 84 days. Hair density was measured on a fixed
area of scalp at day 0 and day 84, and the change was multiplied across the scalp area used in the
study. The best result in the study, scaled that way, is 28,200 new hairs. That is the "up to".
What "up to" means
It means the best responder. It does not mean you. In every hair study a few people respond strongly,
most respond moderately, and some barely respond. We print the top because that is what the rules
allow and what every brand does. We are writing this page because we think you should see the
middle too. When the full dataset is cleared for publication we will put the median on this page. Until
then: the typical responder sees fewer hairs on the pillow by week six and visible new growth at the
hairline by week twelve. That is the result we design for.
Why 84 days
A hair follicle has a cycle. The growth phase is long, the resting phase is around three months, and the
shedding phase is short. Any product that claims a result in two weeks is talking about breakage or
shine, not growth. Twelve weeks is the shortest window in which new growth can be measured
honestly. That is why every number we print is tied to 84 days, and why our routine is sold as an 84-
day course.
What this serum is and is not
It is an actives serum. The same class as the ₹550 to ₹1,100 serums from the other D2C brands, with
the difference that ours comes with a measured result instead of a percentage on the label. It is not
minoxidil. It will not create dependency and it does not need a prescription. It is also not MitoActive,
which works on the follicle's energy supply through Y100 and is the next tier up at ₹1,499 (read:
