WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In a software company this discipline produces the product marketing context document: the file
every other marketing task reads before it writes anything. For Arata, the same file is the brand
positioning and messaging house. Same job. It makes sure the shampoo bottle on Blinkit, the serum
PDP on arata.in, the Amazon bullets and the WhatsApp reply all say the same thing, in the same
words, with the same proof.
Arata is mid-rebrand. The brand started in 2017 as a flaxseed hair gel with a "zero chemicals" identity.
In 2026 it calls itself the "Hair & Scalp Science Co." and ships MitoActive, a ₹1,499 mitochondrial
serum licensed from Yuva Biosciences. That is a big move and the right one. The problem is that the
old brand is still live on the shelves. Nykaa still lists the Super Shampoo with onion and biotin.
Marketplaces still carry the Advanced Curl Care range from the Taapsee Pannu era. The same serum
is called a "serum" in one place and a "tonic" in another. Three brand lines ("systems, not symptoms",
"Good science for great hair", "Zero guesswork") are in circulation at once. A customer who meets
Arata on Amazon, then on Instagram, then on Zepto, meets three slightly different brands.
That costs real money. 68% of FY25 revenue came through marketplaces and quick commerce,
where listing copy is written by whoever is closest to the account manager that week. The 32% on
arata.in has 2,248 reviews and a clear science story, but the hero of the rebrand, MitoActive, launched
in August with zero reviews and a price that is 2.6X the next serum, with no written answer to "why is
this ₹1,499 when the Intensive Serum is ₹571?" Every new ad, listing and PDP is positioned from
scratch, and the claims drift: "7.4X hair fall reduction" is a tensile-strength number, "78% stronger in 1
wash" is in-vitro, "28,200 new hairs" is an "up to" figure. Used loosely, those numbers become the
next "onion shampoo" in the customer's mind. Meanwhile Traya spends ₹138 Cr a year telling India
that hair fall needs a diagnosis, a kit and a doctor.
The opportunity is that nobody in the ₹350 to ₹700 band owns "proof on the pack without the
commitment". Pilgrim and Minimalist sell ingredient percentages. Traya and Man Matters sell diagnosis
and dependency. Minoxidil sells evidence with side effects. Arata already has the product truth to sit

Rules of the ladder:
    Each rung has one sentence and one number. The sentence travels unchanged to Amazon, Nykaa,
    Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart and arata.in.
    Rung 2 and Rung 3 are never on the same page without the "when to upgrade" line. The absence
    of that line is why the ₹1,499 looks arbitrary today.
    Pack of 3 is renamed in copy as "the 84-day course". The 49% discount stays, but the lead
    message is the course.
    MitoActive combos (₹1,803 to ₹2,278) are sold as "MitoActive routine", never as a discount bundle.

EXAMPLE 5: LAB NOTES EXPLAINER, "WHAT 84 DAYS MEANS"
A 180-word Lab Notes post that becomes the link behind every "84 days" claim across channels.
Why every Arata hair growth claim says 84 days
Hair grows in cycles. A follicle spends years in the growth phase (anagen), a few weeks winding
down, then a few months resting (telogen) before it sheds and restarts. Hair fall you can see is usually
too many follicles sitting in telogen at once.
Anything that works on that cycle needs time to show. The earliest you can honestly measure a
change in the growth-to-rest ratio is around 12 weeks. That is 84 days. It is why our Intensive Serum
study ran to 84 days, why the MitoActive study with Indian participants reported at 84 days, and why
we call the Pack of 3 an 84-day course.
What to expect along the way: less hair in the drain by week 4 to 6, baby hairs at the hairline around
week 8, visible density at week 12. Take a photo on day 1 and day 84 in the same light. If you do not
see a difference, message us.

APPENDIX
THE "WRITE FROM THIS" CHECKLIST
Every new ad, listing, PDP, email, script or post passes these eight checks before it ships.
1. Which pillar is this asset built on? Name it.
2. Which persona is it for? Use that row of the message map.
3. Which SKU and which price? Use the locked name.
4. Which claim? Use the long or short form from the ledger. Never the banned form.
5. Does "up to" and the time window appear wherever the number appears?
6. Is the next rung of the ladder visible when a serum is shown?
7. Is the most relevant anxiety answered on the surface (leak, quantity, sameness, forever)?
8. Does it avoid the "avoid" list: chemical-free, miracle, cure, clinical-strength, Korean?

    The document carries a version number and a changelog, newest first, one line per revision: what
    changed and why.
    Every substantive change bumps the version. Typo fixes do not.

    (fortnightly verbatim pull from Loox, Nykaa and Amazon); a new SKU; a competitor move that
