WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This document applies our paid media playbook, which was written for software, to a shampoo and
serum brand: a "qualified lead" becomes a first order from a new customer, "pipeline" becomes the
45-day reorder, and the landing page is a product page or a routine bundle page.
Arata is already a paid-media business. The brand did ₹61.1 Cr in FY25, up 192%, with 32% of that on
arata.in and the other 68% on Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart. The stated goal is
a ₹160 Cr run-rate. Nobody triples a D2C haircare brand on organic reach alone, and the one close
comparable in the category, Traya, spent ₹138 Cr on marketing to make ₹343 Cr. The question is what
each rupee of Arata's spend buys, and whether the brand knows the answer on a Monday morning.
From the outside, three things stand out. First, the ad-to-page journey is weaker than the product. The
hair growth collection mixes claims, there is no routine builder, no quiz, and no comparison page for
the exact searches people type before buying ("minoxidil alternative", "traya vs"). Ads will land on
pages that were built for browsing, so the spend pays for the click and then loses the scent. Second,
MitoActive, the ₹1,499 hero the rebrand is built around, has zero reviews. Running cold traffic to a
zero-review ₹1,499 serum is the most expensive thing a paid account can do. Third, the brand's
strongest asset for ads, a number on every bottle, is barely used as an ad hook. "7.4X", "28,200",
"95% in 1 wash", "87% in 84 days" are headlines. Most competitors in the ₹400 to ₹700 band (Pilgrim,
Minimalist, Bare Anatomy) sell ingredient percentages. Arata can sell results.
What this costs today: a website that is 32% of revenue when it could be 40%+, a hero SKU with no
social proof loop, and 68% of sales on rented land where the margin and the customer data belong to
someone else. Every order moved from a marketplace to arata.in is worth more twice: once in margin,
once in a phone number we can bring back at day 35.
The opportunity is a system, with a target cost per new customer, a weekly decision rule, and creative
that does the targeting. This document sets the number (₹450), the structure (two Meta CBOs, four
Google campaigns, one YouTube layer), the creative (built in the Worked examples), and the 30-day
plan to get it live.

One compliance note that shapes every line of copy. Meta's personal-attributes policy means an ad
must never assert that the viewer has a condition ("your hair is falling"). We write about the product,
the study, and "people who", never "you have". On the Indian side, the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act
bans advertising that claims to cure the conditions in its schedule, and the 2020 draft amendment
proposed adding hair loss and greying. So every claim is written as the study result ("up to", "in 84
days", "in a consumer study"), never as a cure, never as "regrow", never as "baldness".

PHASE 0, WEEK 1: TRACKING AND THE REAL NUMBERS
What: Rebuild measurement before spending. Shopify's native Meta integration with Conversions API
and a shared event ID, Google tag with Enhanced Conversions, GA4 linked with auto-tagging, one
UTM scheme ( utm_source=meta|google|youtube , utm_campaign = campaign name, utm_content = ad
name). A custom new_customer_purchase event in both platforms, so bidding can optimise to new
customers rather than to anyone. Customer list (1.5M customers a year) uploaded as hashed emails
and phone numbers to both platforms. Purchasers in the last 30 days excluded from prospecting.
Why: Every decision rule below keys off TCPO, and TCPO is meaningless if purchases double count or
if the account is optimising to returning buyers who would have bought anyway.
Expected effect: A trustworthy cost per new customer by day 14. Match quality score above 6 on
Meta.

PHASE 1, WEEKS 1 TO 2: GOOGLE, BOTTOM OF THE LADDER FIRST
What: Four Search campaigns with independent budgets. Brand on exact and phrase, capped at about
10% of Google spend. Non-brand high-intent with four themed ad groups (serum, shampoo, dandruff,
grey), phrase and exact only, Maximise Conversions until each campaign clears 30 purchases a
month, then Target ROAS. Competitor and alternative group with its own budget and its own pages.
Shopping through a Merchant Center feed with Loox ratings synced, free-shipping-over-₹299 and
gift-over-₹999 as promotions, and feed titles rewritten to lead with the query ("Hair Growth Serum for
Hair Fall, 30 ml, Arata" rather than "Arata Hair Growth Intensive Serum"). Performance Max only in
week 4, with brand exclusions and a new-customer acquisition goal, and only after Search has 30
days of data.

Why: Brand is cheap and converts; left in a shared budget it eats everything and makes the account
look better than it is. Non-brand is where the ₹160 Cr comes from.
Expected effect: Brand CTR above 10%, non-brand purchase conversion rate 3% to 6% on serum and

PHASE 1, WEEKS 1 TO 2: META, TWO CBOS OVER THE SAME BROAD AUDIENCE
What: A Scaling CBO holding only proven ads, and a Testing CBO with its own protected 20% of
budget. Both target India broad, 22 to 45, with purchasers (30 days) excluded. Twelve statics in the
first batch, long-form primary text, one persona per ad, identity keyword in the headline. Video only
after a static passes. Partnership ads from creator handles in week 3 (the Samay Raina podcast
cutdowns are the obvious first test if the usage rights allow paid amplification).
Why: Inside one CBO, winners starve tests. The separation protects the testing budget. Statics are
cheaper to make, cheaper to deliver, and let us ship 12 concepts in the time a single polished video
takes.
Expected effect: 2 to 3 proven ads by day 30, graduated into Scaling.

PHASE 2, WEEKS 2 TO 3: THE PAGES THE ADS DESERVE
What: Three landers, built by our team, owned by Arata. /pages/84-days , an advertorial that walks
through the 84-day study for the Intensive Serum and MitoActive, with the Pack of 3 at ₹999 as the
natural buy. /pages/minoxidil-alternative , a comparison page for rung 3 searches (price per month,
time to result, prescription needed, dependency, side effects, with Arata's serums alongside minoxidil
5% and the diagnosis-kit model). A routine bundle page for Ritika's cart (Trio ₹1,201). Each page's H1
mirrors the winning Meta headline once we have one.
Why: Meta tests a headline on a thousand times the audience that reaches the page. When the page
restates the exact promise, the click converts. Our expectation from the headline-mirror discipline is a
15% to 20% lift in page conversion on paid traffic.
Expected effect: The cheapest ROAS improvement in the plan, because it costs page work rather than
media.

PHASE 2, WEEK 3: THE RETARGETING LAYER, FOUR ADS AT ONCE
What: All four retargeting ads run together against site visitors (7 to 30 days) and engagers who did
not buy.
1. Objection ad: the ₹1,499 and the leak objections answered in the headline (built in Worked
   examples).
2. Proof carousel: five verbatim reviews from the 2,248 on site, one per card.
3. Other-offer CBO: people who viewed the serum and did not buy see the ₹359 shampoo or the ₹199
   scalp massager. A lower-ticket door into the same routine.
4. Value-first ad: the 84-day plan on WhatsApp, free, before any purchase. It builds the reorder list
   from day one.
Catalog ads to cart abandoners at 1 to 7 days, frequency allowed to run higher.

Why: The main reason someone did not buy is that the offer was wrong for them. Showing the same
serum harder does not fix that. A different product, a proof stack, or a free plan does.
Expected effect: Retargeting ROAS above 4x within 30 days, and a WhatsApp list that the reorder flow

PHASE 3, WEEKS 3 TO 4: REORDER AND LAPSED
What: META_Reorder_D35 : purchasers of the 30 ml serum or the 200 ml shampoo, 30 to 45 days after
purchase, shown a refill ad for the same product with the Pack of 3 as the upgrade. Google customer-
match list of the same cohort with a bid-up on brand. META_Lapsed_D90 : purchasers at 90 to 180 days,
shown a different product than they bought.
Why: The brief names repeat within 45 days as the key conversion, and a 30 ml bottle at twice-daily
use runs out at about day 30. The study window is 84 days. The customer who stops at day 30 never
sees the result they paid for, and never reviews it.
Expected effect: The 30% assumed 90-day repeat moves toward 40%, which raises the 90-day CAC
ceiling from ₹686 to about ₹740 and funds more prospecting.

The ad-count ceiling, (daily budget × 14) ÷ (2 × TCPO), is in the thousands at a D2C TCPO, so it never
binds. The binding constraint for Arata is creative supply. We plan for it: 12 new statics every two
weeks, win rate about 1 in 6, so 2 new winners a fortnight. Each proven ad carries roughly ₹3 lakh a
month before it fatigues (assumption, to be measured), so ₹18 lakh a month on Meta needs six proven
ads in rotation. Budget cannot run ahead of that number.

Decision rules:
    If new-customer CPO is at or under ₹450 for two consecutive weeks on a line, that line gets +20%
    every five days until frequency passes 3.0 or CPO passes ₹675, then we add seed audiences
    (partnership ads, lookalikes from the top-LTV customer list) before adding more money.
    If CPO sits between ₹450 and ₹675 for four weeks on a line, we hold budget flat and run the
    creative and page tests harder. A CPO spike is a question, never a verdict; we check sample size,
    learning phase and conversion lag before pausing anything.
    If CPO is over ₹675 for four weeks on a line, with tracking verified, we cut that line by 30%, and
    move the money to the lines that are working. If the whole prospecting layer is over ₹675 for eight
    weeks, we stop scaling and go back to the pages, the offer and the first-order product before
    buying more media.
    If repeat at 45 days stays under 20% on paid cohorts by day 90, the problem is downstream of ads
    and we shift effort to the reorder and routine work rather than lowering TCPO.
    Every month: recalibrate TCPO against real margin and real repeat, audit the creative library, and sit
    with the founders for the three-hour review. The confidence to scale comes from the founders
    seeing the numbers themselves.
