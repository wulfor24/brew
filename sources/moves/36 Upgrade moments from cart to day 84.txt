WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In software this discipline is the paywall: the screen that asks a free user to pay, shown after they have
felt the product work. For Arata the same discipline is the upgrade moment: the PDP toggle that turns
one bottle into Routine on Repeat, the cart drawer that turns a serum into a routine, the day-25
message that turns a finished bottle into a course, and the day-56 message that turns an Intensive
Serum customer into a MitoActive customer. Same rule applies. Ask after value. Make the next step
obvious. Leave the door open.
Arata has the ladder and no stairs. The catalogue runs from a ₹199 scalp massager to a ₹1,499
MitoActive serum, with combos saving 7% to 49%, free shipping above ₹299 and a free gift above
₹999. Those are five upgrade offers. None of them is presented as a moment. The customer finds the
pack of 3 if they scroll, finds the free-gift threshold if they read the shipping bar, and never finds a
subscription because there is none. With 50,000 D2C orders a month and an AOV near ₹900 to
₹1,000, every missing moment is a few rupees of AOV multiplied by 50,000.
The one upgrade ask Arata does push hard is the wrong one at the wrong time. The Hair Growth
Intensive Serum Pack of 3 at ₹999 is the deepest discount in the catalogue, 49% off MRP, and it is
offered to a first-time visitor who has never used the serum. That is the paywall shown before the aha
moment. It converts some (388 reviews), but it converts by discount rather than by proof, it kills the
reorder for 90 days, and it trains the buyer that ₹333 is the real price. The customers who would
happily pay for a course are the ones who have finished one bottle and seen less hair on the pillow.
Nobody asks them.
The second gap is MitoActive. It launched in August 2026 at ₹1,499 with zero reviews, and the only
path to it is a combo at ₹1,803 to ₹2,278 on a collection page. Arata has 474 reviews on the ₹571
Intensive Serum and a built-in audience of people who have done 56 or 84 days with it. That audience
is the tier-upgrade moment. It is not being asked.

FREQUENCY AND DISMISSAL RULES
The anti-patterns in this discipline are well known: hidden close buttons, pre-ticked subscriptions,
guilt copy, stacked modals. Arata's rules, written down so every app and every developer follows
them:
    One ask per surface. The drawer shows one card. The cart page shows one bar. The post-
    purchase page shows one item. Checkout shows nothing.
    Dismissed means dismissed. "Just the serum" in the drawer suppresses the routine ask on the cart
    page for that session and for 14 days.



BREW DEPLOYMENT TEAM × ARATA · UPGRADE MOMENTS FROM CART TO DAY 84                                      5
    Repeat is never pre-ticked. The one-time price is always visible at the same size as the Repeat
    price.
    WhatsApp carries at most one upgrade message per 14 days per customer. LATER means 10 days
    of silence. STOP ends promotional messages and keeps order messages.
    No countdowns, no "only 3 left", no "others are viewing this". The brand line is "Zero guesswork".
    Pressure contradicts it.
    The replacement promise (leaks, broken seals) sits inside the Repeat terms, because that is the
    fear that stops a subscription.

WHERE MARKETPLACE AND QUICK-COMMERCE BUYERS ENTER THE LADDER
68% of revenue arrives from Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart. Those customers
never see a PDP toggle. Their first upgrade moment is the box. A card in every marketplace and quick-
commerce order carries a QR to a Repeat landing page with the per-day price and the WhatsApp opt-
in. The Repeat price is website only, which gives the marketplace buyer a reason to move. Expected
effect: this is slow and small at first, 1% to 2% of marketplace orders scanning in 90 days
(assumption), but it is the only lever that moves revenue off rented land without cutting marketplace
price parity.
