WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Every hero claim Arata makes has a clock on it. 28,200 new hairs within 84 days. 87% saw growth
with MitoActive in 84 days. 86% fewer greys over 3 months. The claim is a promise about what
happens if the customer keeps going. Today nothing on the brand side keeps them going. There is no
subscription, no routine builder, no app and no community. A customer buys the ₹571 Hair Growth
Intensive Serum, the box arrives, and the next thing they hear from Arata is a marketplace ad. The 84
days happen in silence.
That silence is expensive. The brief puts beauty repeat rates at around 26% and routine-led models
above 40%. Arata does 50,000+ D2C orders a month at an implied AOV of ₹900 to ₹1,000. If
messaging moves the 45-day repeat rate by even 5 points on D2C alone, that is 2,500 extra orders a
month, roughly ₹20 to ₹25 lakh of monthly revenue that does not need a single new customer. The
customer who got the number on the bottle and then stopped at week three is the biggest leak in the
business, bigger than the leaking bottles people complain about in reviews.
The channel for this in India is WhatsApp, with SMS behind it. Arata's buyer is 22 to 40, internet-
native, orders on Blinkit at 11pm and lives inside WhatsApp. Marketing SMS in India now runs through
the TRAI DLT system: registered headers, pre-approved templates, a 10am to 9pm window, and DND
scrubbing that silently removes a large share of any promotional list. WhatsApp runs through Meta's
template system with its own rules, but it reaches the DND customer, carries images and buttons, and
lets the customer reply. So the design is: WhatsApp for the relationship, SMS for OTPs, order fallbacks
and the small promotional slice that has given DLT-grade consent.
There is a second reason this discipline matters more for Arata than for most D2C brands. 68% of
revenue sits on marketplaces and quick commerce. Arata gets no phone number from a Zepto order.
The only object that travels from Arata to that customer is the box. A QR insert that opens a WhatsApp

Three Meta rules shape the design:
1. Cart reminders are marketing, even when the customer asked to be reminded. So are routine
   check-ins that mention a product. Only pure order and account messages qualify as utility. If we
   add a coupon to a shipping update, Meta recategorises it as marketing and we pay the marketing
   rate.
2. Outside the 24-hour window after a customer's last message, only approved templates can go out.
   The QR insert and every reply button are there to open that window, because inside it we can send
   free-form messages, photos and lists.
3. Messaging limits are shared across the business portfolio and start at 250 unique users per day.
   Arata needs 10,000 within the first month and 100,000 by month 3. That only happens by sending
   high-quality templates to people who read them, so the warm-up order matters: recent buyers first,
   cold list last.

PRIORITIES, IN BUILD ORDER
Priority 1: Order utility and the seal check. Cheapest messages, highest read rate, and they warm
the number with people who want to hear from Arata. The seal check on delivery day is the single
message most likely to change Arata's review profile. Expected effect: fewer packaging complaints
reaching Nykaa and Amazon, a growing list of customers who have replied (and so opened a window),
and a green quality rating going into week 3.
Priority 2: The 84-day routine journey. This is the product. Seven touches tied to the claim window,
each with one reply button that either opens a conversation or places a reorder. Expected effect: a
measurable lift in 45-day repeat on D2C, the first bank of MitoActive reviews, and a stream of before-
and-after photos for ads.
Priority 3: Abandoned cart. Arata's 650K monthly visits and 7 to 8% visit-to-order rate imply tens of
thousands of carts a month that never close. Expected effect: 5 to 10% of reminded carts recovered,
with no discount on the first touch.
Priority 4: Reorder and replenishment outside the journey. For customers who bought before the
system existed, and for the Pack of 3 buyers whose clock runs 90 days. Expected effect: repeat lift on
the existing base, which is where most of Arata's 1.5M annual customers sit.
Priority 5: Launch and festive broadcasts. Two marketing broadcasts a month at most. MitoActive to
the serum buyers who have finished 84 days; Diwali to the whole opted-in file.
Priority 6: SMS promotional layer. Only after the DLT consent file is built. One send a month to the
consented, non-WhatsApp segment.

Exits and caps: any purchase pauses reorder nudges for 25 days and restarts the clock. "Stop offers"

any 5-day period, which means a journey touch always wins over a broadcast when they collide.

OPT-IN DESIGN
Checkout, unchecked by default, directly under the phone field:

   [ ] SEND ME ORDER UPDATES AND MY 84-DAY ROUTINE REMINDERS ON
   WHATSAPP AND SMS FROM ARATA. OPT OUT ANYTIME.


Meta prefers that order updates and offers are named separately, and TRAI wants promotional
consent to be explicit. So we store two flags per customer: wa_updates (utility, set when they give a
number for delivery) and wa_marketing (set only by the checkbox, the QR chat, or the CTWA ad
conversation). SMS promotional consent is a third flag, sms_promo , and is pushed to the DLT consent
registry.
The QR insert in every box, including every marketplace and quick-commerce box, says:

   SCAN TO START YOUR 84 DAYS. WE'LL TELL YOU HOW TO USE IT, WHEN
   TO REORDER, AND WHAT TO EXPECT EACH WEEK. IF ANYTHING ARRIVED
   BROKEN, TELL US HERE AND WE REPLACE IT.


The pre-filled first message is "Hi Arata, starting my 84 days with [SKU]". The customer's message
opens a 24-hour window, which means the welcome, the usage video and the opt-in question are all
free-form and free of charge.
