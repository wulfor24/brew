WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This playbook was written for websites in general, including SaaS. The D2C translation is simple:
Arata's "app page" is a Shopify product page, its "feature page" is a collection page, its "docs" are
Lab Notes, and its "pricing table" is the MRP, sale price, free shipping above ₹299 and free gift above
₹999 that Google reads straight off the page through structured data.
Arata gets about 650K visits a month and 32% of revenue from arata.in. Most of those visits start on
Google with a query like "hair fall shampoo", "hair growth serum" or "anti dandruff shampoo". On that
results page, Arata sits next to Pilgrim, Minimalist, Traya and Amazon listings of Arata's own products.
The listing that shows a price, a star rating, stock status and a clean brand name wins the click. Today
that listing is not under Arata's control. It is whatever Shopify's theme and the Loox review app emit by
default, and those two often disagree.
The brand's whole positioning is "a number on every bottle". Structured data is how that number
reaches a search engine. 7.4X, 28,200, 87% in 84 days: none of it is machine-readable right now.
Google reads a product name and a price. An AI assistant asked "which Indian hair growth serum has
a clinical study" has no structured way to find that Arata's Hair Growth Intensive Serum claims up to
28,200 new hairs in 84 days, so it answers with Pilgrim or Minimalist, whose pages are at least
consistent.
Three gaps in the brief make this urgent. MitoActive, the ₹1,499 hero, has zero reviews, which means
review apps will emit an empty or zero rating that can trigger a Search Console error on the most
important page on the site. Naming is inconsistent across channels (onion versus biotin, serum versus
tonic), so Google's product knowledge graph sees several half-matching entities instead of one brand.
And 68% of revenue sits on marketplaces and quick commerce, so when a customer searches a
product name, Amazon and Nykaa listings often outrank arata.in for Arata's own SKUs. A Brand entity,
product identifiers and merchant listing data give arata.in the strongest claim to its own products.
The opportunity is cheap and permanent. One Liquid snippet, deployed once, covers every current
and future SKU. There is no media cost. The effect compounds with every page the content team
publishes and every review Loox collects.

THE PLAN
The whole system is one Liquid snippet ( arata-schema.liquid ) rendered in theme.liquid , with a small
JSON settings file for brand constants and a metafield per product for the claim sentence. Nothing is
hand-pasted per page. Four phases, in priority order.

PHASE 1: FOUNDATION (WEEK 1)
What: an @graph with Organization , Brand and WebSite on every page, plus BreadcrumbList built
from the Shopify collection and product handles.
Why: Google and AI assistants need one authoritative node that says "Arata is a brand, owned by Slick
Organics Private Limited, New Delhi, here are its official profiles and store listings". The sameAs array
points at the Amazon brand store, Nykaa brand page, Flipkart, Instagram and YouTube so the
marketplace listings get linked to the brand instead of competing with it.
Effect: site name and logo in branded results, a cleaner knowledge panel over time, breadcrumbs
replacing the raw URL on every product result. Breadcrumbs alone turn arata.in › products › anti-
hair-fall-intensive-shampoo into Arata › Hair Growth › Anti-Hair Fall Intensive Shampoo .



PHASE 2: PRODUCT PAGES (WEEKS 1 TO 2)
What: one Product node per PDP, built from Shopify's product object. Price from the selected variant,
MRP as ListPrice , InStock or OutOfStock from inventory, GTIN from the variant barcode field, brand
pointing to the Brand node, shippingDetails and hasMerchantReturnPolicy from the settings file.
 aggregateRating reads the Loox metafield and emits only when the count is above zero. Sized SKUs
(Hair Gel 50 ml and 100 ml, styling range) become a ProductGroup with each size as a hasVariant
Product. Combos add isRelatedTo for their components.
Why: this is where the revenue is. Product snippets and merchant listings are the two appearances
that put price and stars under "hair fall shampoo" queries. The guard protects MitoActive from an error
until it has reviews, and the day it crosses one review the stars switch on without a deploy.
Effect: 100% of PDPs eligible for product snippets; PDPs with reviews (Growth Serum 474, Pack of 3
388, Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo 222, Anti-Dandruff Shampoo ~300) show stars within one to two crawl
cycles. Free listings in the Shopping tab for every SKU with correct ₹ pricing.

PHASE 3: COLLECTIONS AND LAB NOTES (WEEKS 2 TO 3)
What: CollectionPage with an ItemList of the products in display order on /collections/hair-growth,
/collections/anti-dandruff and so on. Article on every Lab Notes post with a named author (the in-
house R&D lead or a trichologist, as the content team decides), the Organization as publisher,
 dateModified kept current, and about linking to the Product the post explains.

Why: the collection markup tells Google that "hair growth" at Arata means these six SKUs in this order,
which supports the collection page ranking for the category query rather than a single PDP. The
Article markup gives the seven-posts-in-March burst a chance at Article results and Discover, and
about ties the Y100 explainer to MitoActive so the claim context travels with the product.


Effect: collection pages become the landing page for category queries; Lab Notes posts carry an

PHASE 4: SEMANTIC LAYER AND MONITORING (WEEKS 3 TO 4)
What: VideoObject on any post or PDP with a self-hosted or YouTube video (the Samay Raina podcast
cut, routine explainers). FAQPage on PDP FAQ sections, declared for AI assistants and the Schema.org
validator only, with a note in the settings file that it earns no Google appearance. A weekly Search
Console check of the Product snippets, Merchant listings, Breadcrumbs and Article reports, with any
error or warning spike triggering a fix within 48 hours.
Why: the video and FAQ markup cost nothing once the snippet exists and give AI answer engines
clean question and answer pairs to quote ("Is MitoActive a minoxidil alternative?"). Monitoring is what
keeps the system honest when a price changes or a review app updates.
Effect: zero Search Console errors held as a standing state; AI assistants quoting Arata's own answers
instead of a marketplace listing.

IMPLEMENTATION RULES ON SHOPIFY
1. Disable the theme's built-in product JSON-LD (in Dawn it lives in main-product.liquid under a
    structured_data block). One source only.

2. Turn off "inject rich snippets" in the Loox app settings so it stops emitting its own Product . Keep
   Loox for review collection and read its loox.avg_rating and loox.num_reviews metafields into our
   node.
3. Escape every < as < in the rendered JSON so a product description containing a tag cannot break
   the script element.
4. Shopify's /products.json is the canonical list of SKUs for QA. We diff it against the pages carrying
    Product markup each week; any SKU missing markup is a bug.

5. The claim sentence per SKU lives in a product metafield ( arata.claim_sentence ) so it is written
   once, shown on the page, and copied into description . Marketing changes it in one place.
6. Prices in schema always match Merchant Center. If Arata runs a sale on arata.in, the snippet picks
   it up from the variant price automatically; nobody edits schema for a sale.

Decision rule. At day 90 we look at three things: zero errors held, stars visible on reviewed PDPs, and
CTR on rich-result queries. If all three hold, the system is done and moves to the weekly monitoring
loop, and we extend it to ingredient and comparison pages as those launch. If stars are visible but CTR
has not moved, the issue is the snippet text, and we rewrite titles and meta descriptions on the top 20
PDPs, which is a different discipline, with the schema left as is. If errors recur, the cause is almost
always a theme or app update re-injecting markup, and the fix is to lock the snippet in the theme and
re-audit app settings; we do not remove supported markup to make a report go quiet.
What does not count as a failure: FAQ or HowTo markup not appearing in the Rich Results Test. Those
appearances are retired, the markup is there for AI assistants, and the Schema.org validator is the test
for it.
