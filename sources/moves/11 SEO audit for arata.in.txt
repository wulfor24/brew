WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This discipline applies to you directly. Arata is a Shopify store with a blog, a catalogue of about 40 live
SKUs and a rebrand in progress. The audit below treats arata.in as the asset, Google India as the
channel, and the Shopify defaults as the first thing to fix.
Here is what we see. You get around 650K visits a month and the number is growing about 42%
month on month. That growth line matches a brand buying attention: the Samay Raina campaign,
creators, Meta and quick-commerce placements. Your own site brings in 32% of revenue. The other
68% sits on Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart, where you pay a margin for every
order and own none of the customer. Organic search is the one channel that sends buyers to your own
checkout without a bid, and it compounds. Right now it is under-built.
The queries your customers type are commercial and problem-led: "hair fall shampoo", "hair growth
serum", "anti dandruff shampoo", "anti grey serum", "hair gel". Pilgrim (₹200 Cr+), Traya (₹138 Cr
marketing spend) and Mamaearth outbid you on every one of those terms in paid search. In organic,
the fight is different. Google rewards the page that answers the query best and is linked most. You
have something those brands do not: a number on every bottle, from a study. "7.4X hair fall reduction",
"up to 28,200 new hairs in 84 days", "95% dandruff fungus reduction in 1 wash". Those are the exact
sentences Google pulls into featured snippets and AI Overviews when they sit in the first 100 words of
a page. Today they sit on packaging and in ad creative.
The gaps in your brief map straight onto SEO problems. Naming is inconsistent across channels (onion
vs biotin, serum vs tonic), which on a Shopify store means competing product pages for the same
query. The legacy Curl Care range and the 300 ml Super Shampoo still live on marketplaces and
probably still sit in your sitemap, diluting the science positioning Google reads. Lab Notes publishes in
bursts (seven posts in March 2026), so the blog never builds topical authority on hair fall or dandruff.
MitoActive has zero reviews, so the one product that could own "mitochondrial hair growth" outright
shows no stars in results. The hair growth collection mixes the ₹571 Intensive Serum, the ₹999 pack of
3 and the ₹1,499 MitoActive with no routine logic, so Google cannot tell which page owns "hair growth
serum".

Findings, in the audit format:
Issue 1: Nested collection product URLs carry the internal links. Impact: Medium. Evidence: Shopify
default theme behaviour; verify in crawl. Fix: change the collection grid, cart and recommendation
blocks to link to /products/{handle} directly. One theme change, affects every product. Priority: High.
Issue 2: Legacy products indexed and cannibalising. Impact: High. Evidence: Advanced Curl Care
range (2022), Super Shampoo onion/biotin 300 ml, Happy Hair Duo and Damage Repair Duo still
visible on marketplaces; naming differs by channel. If these product pages are still published on
arata.in, they compete with the Anti-Hair Fall Intensive Shampoo for "hair fall shampoo" and "onion
shampoo". If they were archived without redirects, they are 404s that still hold backlinks and old ad
clicks. Fix: a redirect map (worked example 4) that sends every legacy handle to its successor with a
301. Priority: Critical.
Issue 3: Thin collection pages. Impact: High. Evidence: collections in the brief are organised by
range (Hair Growth, Anti-Dandruff, Damage Defence, Anti-Grey, Styling, Body, Face, Tools, Combos).
A Shopify collection page is a grid with a one-line description. For "anti dandruff shampoo" Google
wants a page that explains the problem, shows the products and answers the follow-up questions. Fix:
300 to 500 words of real content per money collection, above and below the grid, with the claim and
the routine. Priority: High.
Issue 4: Tag and sort URLs. Impact: Low. Evidence: Shopify's default robots.txt blocks
 /collections/*sort_by* , /*+* , /search , /cart , /checkout . Tag pages like /collections/hair-
growth/serum stay crawlable and thin. Fix: add Disallow: /collections/*/* for tag paths in
 robots.txt.liquid only after confirming none of them rank; otherwise noindex them in the theme.
Priority: Low.
Issue 5: Sitemap hygiene. Impact: Medium. Evidence: Shopify auto-generates /sitemap.xml with
product, collection, page and blog children. It includes every published product, including ones you do
not want ranked (loofah, derma roller, legacy packs). Fix: unpublish from the Online Store sales
channel anything that should not rank, keep it on marketplaces. Priority: Medium.
Issue 6: Brand entity ambiguity. Impact: Medium. Evidence: "Arata" is a common Japanese given
name and a river; a site:arata.in lookup returns unrelated entities before the brand. Google has not
clearly resolved the entity. Fix: Organization schema on the homepage with sameAs to Instagram,
YouTube, Amazon brand store, Nykaa brand page, LinkedIn and the Wikipedia-grade press; consistent
"Arata, Hair & Scalp Science Co., New Delhi" naming across every profile. Priority: Medium.

ON-PAGE: TITLES, DESCRIPTIONS, HEADINGS
Shopify's default title is {Product title} – {Store name} . For you that produces titles like "Anti-Hair
Fall Intensive Shampoo – Arata". The query is in the title, which is good. The reason to click is missing,
and the title says nothing a Pilgrim or Mamaearth title does not.
We ran the SERP check the title reference asks for. For "best hair fall shampoo India" the top results
are publisher listicles: Business Insider, PharmEasy, Truemeds, MomJunction and StyleCraze. No
brand page ranks. That tells us two things. A collection page will not win a "best" query, so we do not
chase it. And the product page can win the commercial query "anti hair fall shampoo" where Google
shows product results, if the title names the outcome and the page carries the study.
What we expect the crawl to show on money pages (verify in crawl): titles unique but generic with no
claim or size; meta descriptions auto-filled from the product description and cut mid-sentence; H2s
used for styling ("How to use", "Ingredients") rather than for the questions people type; the claim as a
banner image rather than text in the first 100 words; alt text equal to the product title on every image;
combo pages titled by their contents with no query of their own.

PHASE 1: CRITICAL FIXES (WEEKS 1 TO 2)
1. Redirect map for legacy SKUs. Every unpublished or renamed handle gets a 301 to its successor
   (worked example 4). Why: these pages hold old links and old ad clicks, and they split the hair fall
   query. Effect: consolidated relevance on the live page within one crawl cycle.
2. Canonical internal links. Collection grids, recommendations and cart links point at
    /products/{handle} . Effect: all internal link equity flows to one URL per product.

3. Sitemap and channel cleanup. Products that should not rank come off the Online Store channel;
   tag pages noindexed. Why: crawl budget on a 40-SKU store is fine; the signal of 60 thin pages is
   the problem.
4. Naming freeze. One name per product across arata.in, Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart and quick
   commerce. "Hair Growth Intensive Serum" everywhere, never "tonic". Effect: brand plus product
   queries concentrate on one spelling.

PHASE 2: THE 14 MONEY PAGES (WEEKS 2 TO 3)
The pages: Anti-Hair Fall Intensive Shampoo, Hair Growth Intensive Serum, Pack of 3, MitoActive,
Anti-Breakage Conditioner, Anti-Dandruff Shampoo, Anti-Dandruff Conditioner, Anti-Dandruff Serum,
Damage Defence Super Shampoo, Anti-Grey Hair Serum, Hair Gel, and the Hair Growth, Anti-Dandruff
and Styling collections.
For each page, in this order:
1. Title and meta description from the table in worked example 1
2. H1 aligned with the title, H2s that are the questions people search
3. The claim and the timeline in the first two sentences of body text
4. A five-question FAQ block, marked up as FAQPage, written from the objections in the brief
5. Three verbatim reviews as text
6. Alt text that describes the image, not the product name
7. Internal links: each product links to its hub page, its routine partners and the research page
8. Product schema verified in the Rich Results Test
Why this order: title and first 100 words carry the most ranking weight per minute of work, and the FAQ
block is what gets quoted in AI Overviews. Effect: money pages move into the 8 to 20 band for their
owner queries within six weeks, which makes them candidates for the push loop.

Why hubs: "how to stop hair fall" and "dandruff treatment" are research queries. A product page
cannot answer them and a PharmEasy listicle currently does. A hub that answers in the first two
sentences and routes to the routine captures the searcher one step earlier than any competitor brand

Why ingredient pages: "Y100", "Hairdian AP" and "MelanoGray" have close to zero competition and
you hold the India licence for Y100. These pages rank fast and become the proof layer every product
page links to.
Lab Notes cadence: one post a week, Thursday, each from the keyword map. The first eight titles are
in the appendix.

PHASE 4: REBRAND PROTECTION (WEEK 4 ONWARD)
What: a URL freeze (no handle changes), a redirect map for any page that must move, title and H1
parity checks, a pre-launch crawl of staging, a post-launch crawl within 24 hours. Why: the rebrand is
the single biggest SEO risk in the next two quarters. Effect: organic traffic holds flat or climbs through
the rebrand instead of dropping for a quarter.
