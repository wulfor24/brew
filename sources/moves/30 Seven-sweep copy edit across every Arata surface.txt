WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Arata does not have a copy shortage. It has a copy consistency problem. The brand already owns the
best raw material in the ₹400 to ₹700 haircare band: 7.4X, 28,200, 95%, 86%, 87%, each from a
study. Those numbers appear in four or five different forms depending on where the customer meets
them. The Intensive Serum is "Hair Growth Intensive Serum" on arata.in and "Intensive Hair Growth
Serum" on Nykaa Man and reseller listings. The legacy Super Shampoo is "onion" on one channel and
"biotin" on another. The brief records serum and tonic used for the same product. A customer who
sees three names for one bottle assumes three bottles, or assumes carelessness. Either way the
number on the pack loses its authority.
The second problem is the rebrand. Arata moved from "zero chemicals" to "Hair & Scalp Science Co."
and the live copy still speaks both languages. "Zero guesswork" and "Good science for great hair" sit
next to review themes like "no chemical harm" and "free from hard chemicals". The science voice and
the natural voice are both on the page. A reader hears hedging. Copy editing fixes this faster than a
rewrite: the sentences exist, the claims exist, the job is to make them say one thing in one voice.
The third problem is proof context. "Up to 86% reduction in greys" comes from best responders over
three months. "78% stronger hair in 1 wash" is an in-vitro result. "7.4X hair fall reduction" is a tensile
strength study. None of that context travels with the headline. The sceptic in the brief, the one who
has spent ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 on three to five products that did nothing, reads a bare 86% and discounts
it to zero. The honest frame is also the persuasive frame, and it is one line of copy away.
What this costs: 650K monthly visits convert at an implied 7 to 8% including returning buyers. Each
point of conversion on arata.in is roughly 6,500 orders a month (assumption: visits held flat).
Marketplaces and quick commerce are 68% of revenue, and on those surfaces the only copy Arata
controls is the title, the bullets and the A+ block. A title that flips word order between channels splits
search demand and review equity across listings instead of stacking it on one.

THE RULES THAT PRODUCED THE EDITS
These are the house rules we applied in every worked example. They go into the claims sheet as the
style page.
 1. Number first, method second, window third. "7.4X less hair fall, measured by tensile strength, over
    84 days." The method line is what makes the sceptic stay.
 2. "Up to" stays, and it stays honest. Where the brief says best responders, the copy says best
    responders. Honest framing beats inflated framing, and it is the only framing that survives a
    dermatologist's screenshot.
 3. One name per SKU, everywhere. Format: Arata + concern + product type + hero active in brackets
    + size. "Arata Hair Growth Intensive Serum (Redensyl + Anagain) 30 ml". The word order on arata.in
    wins because it is the channel Arata owns.
 4. Science voice only. Say vegan, cruelty-free, dermatologically tested, no sulphates or parabens in
    shampoos, every active is there for a measured reason. Retire "chemical-free" and "zero
    chemicals" from every surface including legacy listings.
 5. Say what the customer said. Review language is the emotion layer: "hair on the pillow", "flakes on
    black clothes", "without any sticky residue or stiffness". Use it in the line under the number.
 6. Answer the price on the page. ₹1,499 ÷ 30 days = ₹50 a day. That sentence goes wherever the
    price appears.
 7. Answer the leak on the page. The replacement promise is copy, and it belongs on the PDP, the
    order confirmation and the box.
 8. One fragment per section. One list of three per section. No em dashes anywhere.
 9. No contrast reveals, no negation lists. "Systems, not symptoms" becomes an internal line. On the
    page we say what the system is.
10. Mechanism gets one explaining clause the first time it appears. "Y100 helps hair follicle cells make
    more energy, which keeps more hairs in the growing phase." After that, "Y100" alone.

 APPENDIX
 THE TWENTY-MINUTE QUICK PASS (THE COPY GATE)
 Run this on any new line before it goes live on any channel.
 1. Does the line come from the claims sheet? If the SKU has a claim and this line differs, use the
    sheet.
 2. Number first, method second, window third. Is the method there?
 3. Does "up to" appear wherever the brief's study says best responders or top of range?
 4. Is the SKU name in canonical form with size?
 5. Is there a contrast reveal ("not X, Y"), a negation list ("no X, no Y, no Z"), a self-answered question
    or a colon reveal? Rewrite from the fact.
 6. More than one fragment in this section? More than one list of three? Cut one.
 7. Any em dash? Replace with a period, a colon or a line break.
 8. Any of these words: chemical-free, miracle, cure, regrow 100%, ayurvedic, clinical-strength,
    revolutionary, game-changing? Remove.
 9. Is the price near the claim, and for MitoActive is ₹50 a day near the price?
10. Does the customer know what happens if the bottle leaks?
11. Read it aloud once. If you would not say it to a friend who asked about their hair fall, rewrite it.
