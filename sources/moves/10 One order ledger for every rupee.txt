WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This document applies our attribution playbook, written for software companies, to a shampoo and
serum brand. The "CRM as source of truth" becomes the order ledger across Shopify, marketplaces
and quick commerce. The "demo request" becomes a first order. The "sales call that never shows up
in analytics" becomes a Blinkit order placed ten minutes after someone saw a Reel.
Arata has a measurement problem that is shaped like its channel mix. FY25 revenue was ₹61.1 Cr, split
38% marketplaces, 32% arata.in, 30% quick commerce. The brand runs ads on Meta and Google,
pays creators, and spent real money on a Samay Raina mock podcast whose stated goal was brand
recall, in the founders' words "real estate in people's minds". Every one of those investments is
measured by a pixel that lives on arata.in. That pixel sees 32% of the outcome. When Hair-fall Rahul
watches the podcast clip on YouTube, sees a Reel three days later, and orders the Hair Growth
Intensive Serum on Blinkit for ₹571, Meta reports nothing, GA4 reports nothing, Shopify reports
nothing. The sale shows up as "Blinkit grew" in a seller dashboard a week later. The marketing that
caused it looks like it did nothing.
The opposite error happens on the 32% the pixel can see. Meta and Google each claim arata.in orders
inside their own windows, including view-through. On a brand doing 50,000 D2C orders a month, the
two platforms together will routinely report more orders than Shopify processed. Add the GA4 view,
where WhatsApp forwards, Instagram in-app browsers and UPI payment redirects dump orders into
"direct", and the brand is holding three dashboards that each tell a different story. Nobody is lying.
Each one is counting its own way.
What this costs: the ₹160 Cr target needs a marketing budget that will likely run into double-digit
crores a year (Traya spent ₹138 Cr to reach ₹343 Cr). Allocating that budget on platform-reported
ROAS over-funds retargeting and branded search, which collect demand, and under-funds Reels,
YouTube and the culture campaigns, which create it. Over a year that is the difference between buying

unanswerable: if Meta spend rises 30% and Blinkit orders rise 20% in the same month, is that cause
or coincidence? Today Arata cannot say.
The opportunity is unusual. Quick commerce is city-level by nature. Dark stores serve pin codes. Meta
and Google can target and exclude by city. That means Arata can run the one kind of test that answers
"did this channel cause orders on platforms we do not own": hold Meta out of a few cities, read total
orders across all six channels in those cities, and compare. Most D2C brands cannot do this because
their offline sales are not geographically reported. Arata's are. This document builds the ledger, the
survey, the taxonomy and the first test, so the brand can move budget on evidence rather than on
whichever dashboard shouts loudest.

WHERE ARATA'S ORDERS HIDE
     Direct. Bookmarks and typed URLs, yes. Also every WhatsApp forward of a product link, every link
     shared in a family group, every Instagram in-app browser session where the referrer dropped, and
     every customer who saw the podcast and typed arata.in. We expect direct to be large on arata.in.
     That is evidence of a working top of funnel, and we will confirm it with the survey rather than
     assume it.
     Branded search. "arata hair fall shampoo", "arata serum review", "arata mitoactive". People who
     discovered the brand on Reels or YouTube and Googled the name. Last click hands this to Google
     Ads branded campaigns. Until branded and non-branded are split, the podcast and Reels will look
     weak and branded search will look like the best channel in the account.
     Quick-commerce search. "arata" typed into Blinkit or Zepto is the same branded-search
     behaviour, one tap from purchase. Where the platform reports brand search impressions, it is the
     cleanest recall metric Arata has for 30% of its revenue. Where it does not, city-level orders are the
     proxy.
     Dark social. A friend's Instagram story, a WhatsApp voice note saying "try the Arata one", a Reddit
     thread on hair fall. No referrer, ever. Only the survey sees it.
     AI search. "best hair fall serum that is not minoxidil" asked to an assistant, then a branded search.
     Invisible to analytics. We name it here and leave the deeper work to the AI search document.

LAYER 1: ONE LEDGER, CLEAN FIRST TOUCH, BRANDED SPLIT
What. A weekly order table, one row per channel per city per week, deduplicated: Shopify orders by
ship-to city, Amazon/Nykaa/Flipkart by state or city as reported, Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart by city.
Columns: orders, new vs repeat where the platform tells us, revenue, units, hero SKU share




BREW DEPLOYMENT TEAM × ARATA · ONE ORDER LEDGER FOR EVERY RUPEE                                                                   5
(MitoActive, Intensive Serum, Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo, Anti-Dandruff Shampoo). Alongside it, a weekly
spend table by channel and by city where the platform reports it.
Why. Attribution starts with one number for conversions. Today that number is Shopify's for 32% and
a monthly finance roll-up for the rest. Platform claims get deduplicated against the ledger. They never
add to it.
Rule we write down. Never sum platform-reported orders. If Meta claims 31,000 and Google claims
17,500 against 50,000 Shopify orders, Arata has 50,000 orders with overlapping claimants. The ledger
defines "how many". Platforms explain "from where".
Clean first touch (settings, not code). In GA4 referral exclusions and in the analytics tool: Razorpay,
PayU, Cashfree, PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, shop.app and Shopify checkout hosts,
accounts.google.com, any Arata subdomain. Without this, a Reel-driven order whose payment went
through a UPI app can come back with the UPI app as its source. This is the highest-trust-per-effort
fix in the plan. One afternoon.
Branded split. Separate Google Ads campaigns for branded ("arata", misspellings, "arata mitoactive")
and non-branded ("hair fall shampoo", "hair growth serum", "minoxidil alternative"). In reporting,
branded search and direct are grouped as "demand collected", never as channels that create
customers. Branded search impressions from Search Console become a weekly recall KPI, plotted
against culture campaign flights and Reel spend.
Expected effect. Within two weeks Arata has, for the first time, a single page that says how many
orders happened, where, and what was spent. The paid-social share of arata.in orders in GA4 rises
once payment referrers stop stealing credit; direct shrinks by the same amount.

LAYER 2: ASK EVERY BUYER
What. One question at three moments.
1. arata.in thank-you page, right after the order confirmation: "How did you first hear about Arata?"
   Pick-list plus free text. Target 25% response (assumption, typical for a one-question thank-you
   page survey).
2. WhatsApp, 2 days after delivery, for every arata.in customer who opted in: same question, tap-to-
   answer.
3. Pack insert in every unit shipped through quick commerce and marketplaces: a QR to arata.in/start
   that asks "where did you buy this" and "how did you first hear about us", then offers WhatsApp
   opt-in and a reorder benefit. This is the only route to a self-reported read on 68% of orders, and
   the only route to owning that customer's phone number.
Why. On a brand whose founders explicitly spend on recall, the survey is the primary measurement of
that spend. It is also the tiebreaker when Meta and Google claim the same arata.in order.
How we use it. Survey share is evidence of awareness, never a credit rule. If 18% of respondents say
YouTube, that tells us YouTube is working and that YouTube should earn a bounded override in
reporting (Layer 3), after a holdout confirms direction. We discount survey share, we do not paste it
into the allocation.

Expected effect. A monthly "where customers come from" chart that includes podcast, YouTube,
friends, Reddit and "saw it on Blinkit", none of which any pixel reports. The pack insert adds a
WhatsApp number from a share of quick-commerce buyers (assumption: 3% to 6% scan rate), which
is the first owned relationship with the 68%.

LAYER 3: SOURCE ON THE RECORD, FULL TOUCH PATH
What. Three fields on every customer in Shopify (as tags) and in the WhatsApp and email tool:
     source   : the channel, from our taxonomy below
     source_basis  : journey_linked (tracked click path on arata.in), self_reported (survey or pack
    insert), code (creator or affiliate coupon), campaign_window (fallback: date, landing page and active
    UTMs narrow it to one source), or unknown
     source_confidence : high, medium, low, computed from evidence quality, not just basis. A
    journey_linked click with clean UTMs is high. The same with a missing medium is medium. A
    specific self-report ("Samay Raina podcast") is high. "Social media" is low.
Plus acquisition_cost_class : paid (cpc, paid-social, display, sponsored creator, quick-commerce
sponsored listing), owned (WhatsApp, email, pack insert), earned (organic search, organic social, PR,
podcast, word of mouth), marketplace_organic , direct_unknown .
And the ordered touch path per customer: channel and timestamp for every tracked touch on arata.in,
stored on the customer, so position-based and time-decay can be scored on Arata's own data instead
of only read in a dropdown.
Why. A breakdown in GA4 is a report. A source on the customer record is something the 45-day
reorder flow can branch on. A pack-insert customer who came from Blinkit gets a different day-35
message from a customer who came from a MitoActive search ad. That is the hand-off to the retention
and email plans.
Identity gap for Arata. Shopify identifies at checkout, so arata.in buyers are already joined to their
browsing session. The un-joined conversions are: WhatsApp opt-ins that happen before purchase
(join on phone number), quiz or routine-builder completions once those exist (join at completion), and
every marketplace or quick-commerce buyer (join only through the pack insert or a later arata.in order
on the same phone number). We instrument only those. We do not rebuild the checkout join that
already works.
Documented override rule. Once the city holdout gives a measured lift for Meta, we add a channel-
level override in the reporting layer: shift a conservative share of "direct" and "branded search"
arata.in orders into Meta for the test window, bounded by the measured lift, never 100% of a noisy
point estimate. The raw table stays untouched. Decision dashboards read the adjusted one. One
channel, one rule, dated.
Expected effect. Every new customer carries a labelled, confidence-rated origin within 30 days. The
share of customers with source_basis = unknown becomes a tracked coverage metric and falls month
on month.

LAYER 4: CITY HOLDOUT TESTS
What. Hold Meta prospecting out of a set of cities for four weeks. Keep everything else running
everywhere. Read total orders across all six channels, by city, by week, before and during. Lift in
exposed cities minus lift in held-out cities is the incremental effect of Meta on all of Arata's orders,
including the ones on Blinkit and Amazon.
Why. This is the only test that answers the question the whole channel mix raises. It also calibrates
platform ROAS: when Meta reports a 4.0 ROAS on arata.in and the holdout shows a 1.8 incremental
blended return across all channels, Arata knows the discount to apply to Meta's number, and knows it
from its own data.
Sequence. Test 1 (week 4 to 8): Meta prospecting, city holdout. Test 2 (week 9 to 12): branded search
paid on/off, alternating two weeks on and two weeks off, measuring total branded clicks (paid plus
organic) and arata.in orders; the question is whether paid branded buys clicks organic would have
delivered. Test 3 (from week 6, continuous): a 10% random holdout on the day-35 WhatsApp reorder
message, so the retention plan is measured on lift rather than on opens.
Expected effect. Three numbers that today do not exist: incremental blended CPA for Meta across all
channels, the cannibalisation rate of paid branded search, and the true lift of the reorder nudge. These
three move more budget correctly than any model.

Question. Does Meta prospecting cause orders across all Arata channels, and at what incremental
cost?
Design. Geo holdout, city level, difference in differences.
Holdout cities (Meta prospecting paused, retargeting and all other channels unchanged): Pune,
Jaipur, Lucknow, Chandigarh tri-city, Kochi. Assumption: together these are 12% to 18% of Arata's all-
channel orders, large enough to read and small enough to afford. Delhi NCR is excluded from holdout

Exposed cities: every other city where Meta prospecting runs today.
Pre-period: 8 weeks of ledger data before the switch, by city and week, all channels. This is why the

Test period: 4 weeks. Starts on a Monday. No price change, no new SKU launch, no festive sale

Illustrative result (assumption). Holdout cities drop 9% week on week versus pre-period. Exposed
