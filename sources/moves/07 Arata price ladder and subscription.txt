WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In SaaS this discipline means tiers, value metrics and willingness-to-pay research. For Arata it means
SKU prices, the bundle architecture, subscription pricing and the MitoActive price ladder. Same
method, different unit: the thing Arata charges for is a day of routine.
Arata's brand line is "systems, not symptoms". The price list says the opposite. The Hair Growth
Intensive Serum Pack of 3 sells at ₹999, which is 49% off MRP and 42% below three bottles at the
₹571 sale price. The Hair Growth Trio (shampoo, conditioner, serum) sells at ₹1,201, which is 7% below
the same three bottles bought one by one. The MitoActive plus shampoo combo saves 3%. The Anti-
Dandruff shampoo plus conditioner combo is listed at ₹866 while the two bottles at their sale prices
add up to ₹853. By the brief's numbers, the customer who buys the system pays more than the
customer who buys the pieces. The customer who buys one product three times gets the deepest
discount in the catalogue.
That ₹333 per-bottle price does three things. It trains every repeat buyer to never pay ₹571 for the
serum again. It halves the serum's perceived value against Pilgrim at ₹595 to ₹699, Minimalist at ₹699
and Bare Anatomy at ₹649, on a product that carries a stronger claim than all of them. And it breaks
the reorder loop for 90 days, because the customer now has three bottles on the shelf and no reason
to come back. With AOV at roughly ₹900 to ₹1,000 and 50,000 D2C orders a month, a discount
structure that points customers away from routines is a revenue leak on the 32% of sales that Arata
owns outright.
The second problem is the MitoActive gap. The ladder runs ₹359, ₹404, ₹449, ₹571, then jumps to
₹1,499. Nothing sits between ₹571 and ₹1,499. A Rahul who has used the Intensive Serum for two
months and wants the next step is asked to pay 2.6 times more for a product with zero reviews. There
is no rung, no course, no "this is what 84 days costs" framing, even though the headline claim (87%
saw growth in 84 days) is itself an 84-day claim.
Third, there is no subscription. The brief says beauty repeat rates sit near 26% and routine-led models
exceed 40%. Arata's stated conversion goal is a repeat purchase within 45 days. Today that repeat
depends on the customer remembering. A subscription priced correctly, with a 30-day serum

The opportunity: hold the single-bottle prices roughly where they are, reorder the discounts so the

that a ₹571 customer can climb. None of this needs a new product. It needs a rulebook and six weeks.

WHAT THE CATALOGUE ALREADY TELLS US ABOUT WILLINGNESS TO PAY

THE VALUE METRIC: A DAY OF ROUTINE
Arata should price and present everything in days. A 30 ml serum is 30 days. A 200 ml shampoo is
about 45 days. An 84-day course is the unit the clinical claims are written in. When every price on the
site reads "₹599 · 30 days · ₹20/day", three things happen: MitoActive stops looking expensive and
starts looking like Traya without the doctor, the pack of 3 stops looking like a bargain bin and starts
looking like a course, and the subscription becomes the obvious way to buy days.

ROUTINE ON REPEAT: THE SUBSCRIPTION
Design rules, in order of importance:
    10% off the sale price, every delivery, no first-order-only trick. ₹9 endings: serum ₹539, MitoActive
    ₹1,349, AHF shampoo ₹319, conditioner ₹319, AD shampoo ₹359, Hair Growth System ₹1,039.

    Cadence set by the product: 30 days for serums, 45 days for washes, 30 days for systems (the
    serum runs out first).
    Skip, pause, swap or cancel from a WhatsApp reply. No login needed. This is what turns the 45-
    day repeat goal into a default.

buyer. 10% plus free shipping plus the gift is the right shape.

THE 84-DAY PROMISE
The pricing page checklist asks for a guarantee near the CTA. MitoActive at ₹3,999 prepaid needs
one. The promise: buy the MitoActive 84-day Course, upload a crown and hairline photo at day 0 and
day 84 through the WhatsApp check-ins, and if you see no change, the third bottle is refunded.
Traya's money-back lives at five months; ours lives at 84 days because the study does. Assumption:
refund rate under 8% of course buyers, which at ₹1,333 per refund is a cost of roughly ₹107 per
course sold, less than the discount we would otherwise need to move a zero-review product.
