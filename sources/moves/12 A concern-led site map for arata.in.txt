WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
arata.in is the one channel Arata owns. It carries 32% of revenue, about 650K visits a month, and
50,000+ D2C orders a month. The other 68% sits on Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart, Zepto, Blinkit and
Instamart, where Arata rents a listing and competes on a sort order it does not control. Every structural
gain on the owned site compounds. Every structural flaw costs orders at 650K visits a month.
Today the site is organised the way the catalogue is organised: by range. Hair Growth, Anti-Dandruff,
Damage Defence, Anti-Grey, Styling, Body, Face, Tools, Combos. That works for a warehouse. It does
not work for Hair-fall Rahul, who typed "hair fall shampoo" into Google, landed on a collection that
mixes a ₹359 shampoo with a 7.4X claim, a ₹571 serum with a 28,200 claim, a ₹1,499 MitoActive with
an 87% claim and a conditioner with a 78% claim, and now has to work out which number is his. The
brief names this: the hair growth collection mixes claims and has no routine builder. There is no quiz.
There is no regimen builder. There is no subscription. The customer has to assemble a routine by
reading.
The brand line is "systems, not symptoms". The site sells symptoms one bottle at a time. That gap
between what the brand says and what the site does is the whole opportunity. A routine-led structure
is also the structure that lifts AOV: the brief implies ₹900 to ₹1,000 AOV against a ₹359 shampoo, so
routines and packs are already doing the work. The site should make the routine the default landing
point, with the single bottle as the step-down.
Three more things are structural and belong in this document. Lab Notes posts arrive in bursts (seven
in March 2026) and sit beside the shop instead of inside it, so they rank for nothing and sell nothing.
Naming is inconsistent across channels (onion vs biotin, serum vs tonic), which means URL handles,
titles, breadcrumbs and marketplace listings all disagree, and search engines see three products
where there is one. And the legacy Advanced Curl Care and Super Shampoo pages are still live and
indexed, pulling the site back to the "zero chemicals" era while the rebrand pushes it toward
bioscience.

DESIGN RULES WE APPLIED
1. Three levels. Home → concern → product. The routine is a product (a Shopify bundle), so it sits at
   the same level as a bottle.
2. The header carries concerns. Ranges go. Six items plus one call to action. Body, Face and Tools
   move to the footer and the mega menu; they are under 10% of the story (assumption based on
   catalogue size and review counts).
3. One name per SKU. The product handle is the name, lowercased with hyphens, and it never
   changes after the rebrand.
4. Every page has a parent and a breadcrumb. No orphans, including every Lab Notes post.
5. Legacy pages redirect, they do not linger.
6. Shopify rails only. The current platform works and the problem is structure, so there is no reverse
   proxy, subdomain or replatform in this plan.
