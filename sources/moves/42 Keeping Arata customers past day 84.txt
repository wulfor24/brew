WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
In SaaS, churn means a subscriber cancels. For Arata, churn means a customer finishes the bottle and
does not buy the next one. We treat the 45-day reorder window as the billing cycle, the "not
reordered" customer as the cancellation, and leaked bottles, failed UPI mandates and COD returns as
the failed payments. Everything below is built on that translation.
Here is the diagnosis. Arata sells results that take 84 days. The Hair Growth Intensive Serum promises
"up to +28,200 new hair within 84 days". MitoActive promises "87% saw increased growth in 84
days". A 30 ml bottle is 30 days of supply at once-daily use, and MitoActive is a one-month supply at
twice-daily use by your own framing. So the product cannot deliver its headline claim unless the
customer buys at least three times. Today, nothing on arata.in makes that happen. There is no
subscription, no routine builder, no reorder reminder, no 84-day check-in. The customer is left to
remember on her own, and Blinkit is one thumb away with a Pilgrim serum on offer.
The cost is large. Arata processes 50,000+ D2C orders a month at an implied AOV of ₹900 to ₹1,000.
Beauty repeat rates sit around 26%; routine-led models exceed 40%. If roughly 30,000 of those
monthly orders are first orders (assumption), every point of repeat rate is about 300 extra orders a
month, or ₹2.8 lakh a month at ₹950 AOV. Fourteen points, the gap between a category-average
beauty brand and a routine brand, is roughly ₹40 lakh a month, close to ₹5 Cr a year, from customers
you have already paid to acquire. That is the retention gap, and it sits inside the ₹160 Cr goal.
There is a second, quieter leak. The reviews say "the seal was broken", "the shampoo bottle was
leaking", "it doesn't have the box". A customer who gets a leaking bottle has paid and received
nothing usable. In our model that is a failed payment, and like a failed payment it is the most
recoverable churn you have, because the customer still wants the product. Nobody at Arata is
currently running a recovery loop on it.
The opportunity is that Arata already has the two things most brands lack: a reason to come back (a
dated claim with a number on it) and a routine to sell (shampoo + serum + conditioner, "systems, not
symptoms"). We only need to build the machinery around them. This document is that machinery.

Why: the claim is dated. Using the date as the retention frame turns a reminder into a plan. It also fixes
MitoActive's zero reviews, because the day 84 message is where reviews come from.
Expected effect: 45-day reorder rate for serum buyers moves from category baseline toward 40%,
and MitoActive collects its first 100 reviews inside a quarter.

4. THE RECOVERY LOOP
What: four recovery tracks that run automatically.
    Mandate failure: retry on day 1, day 3, day 5 and day 7; WhatsApp on day 0, 3 and 7; email on day 0
    and 7; grace period of 10 days with the next shipment held; then pause the subscription, never
    delete it.
    COD confirmation: WhatsApp confirm within 10 minutes of a COD order, ₹30 off to switch to
    prepaid, address check on the second attempt, and a hold on repeat COD for any customer with a
    prior RTO.
    Leak and seal: any ticket or review mentioning leak, seal, broken or box triggers a replacement
    order within 24 hours and a WhatsApp from a named support person. This sits above every other
    message in priority.
    Quick-commerce stock-out: when a hero SKU is out of stock on Blinkit or Zepto in a metro
    (assumption: monitored by the ops team weekly), the reorder nudge for customers in that city
    routes to arata.in with same-week delivery and free shipping.
Why: failed payments and failed deliveries are the most recoverable churn there is. The customer still
wants the product.
Expected effect: 50 to 60% of failed mandates recovered; COD RTO down by a third on nudged
orders; leak complaints in reviews fall to near zero within a quarter.

5. THE SCORE AND THE WEEKLY REVIEW
What: the reorder-propensity score computed nightly from Shopify, the WhatsApp provider and the
support desk, and a weekly cohort table cut by first SKU, channel of acquisition, city and month.
Why: everything above has timing assumptions in it. The score and the cohort table are how we
replace assumptions with Arata's real intervals, and how we see which first SKU produces the best
third order.
Expected effect: by week 8 the nudge timings are observed, not assumed, and we know which entry
product deserves the acquisition budget.
