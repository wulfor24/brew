WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Arata already knows how to use famous people. Taapsee Pannu as "CurlFriend" in 2022. The Samay
Raina and Archana Puran Singh mock podcast in February 2026, with the brand name hidden on the
props. Dhruv Madhok has said the goal of that work was "brand recall and cultural familiarity". It
worked as a cultural moment. What it did not build is the thing a hair fall brand needs most: a steady
supply of ordinary people showing, on camera, that the number on the bottle came true for them.
Hair fall is a slow-proof category. The Hair Growth Intensive Serum promises "up to +28,200 new hair
within 84 days". MitoActive says "87% saw increased growth in 84 days". The customer's anxiety, in
the brief's own words, is "Will I waste another ₹600?" and "Will it make hair fall worse first?". A
celebrity cannot answer that. A 28-year-old in Bangalore filming his crown every four weeks can.
Today Arata has 474 reviews on the Intensive Serum, 2,248 reviews on site, and zero reviews on
MitoActive, the ₹1,499 hero the rebrand is built around. The proof gap is largest exactly where the
price is highest.
The competition is already spending here. Traya put ₹138 Cr into marketing to make ₹343 Cr, and a
large share of that is testimonial and creator content that runs as paid ads. Pilgrim and Minimalist own
the Nykaa and Amazon creator feeds with ingredient-percentage content. Arata's edge is that it has a
tested number and the others have a percentage of Redensyl. That edge only shows up if creators are
briefed on the number and allowed to say it in their own words.
What the gap costs: MitoActive at ₹1,499 with no social proof will not convert cold traffic, the 84-day
story is told only by the brand, and every creator post that Arata has paid for so far has been a one-off
with no usage rights, so it did not become an ad. Each of those is fixable in 30 days.
This document sets up four layers that run together: a paid 84-day cohort filmed start to finish, nano
seeding at volume for the shampoos, a dermatologist layer for MitoActive and Y100, and seeding of
MitoActive to Arata's own best reviewers so the hero gets its first honest reviews. Every layer has a
code, a link, a disclosure rule and a decision rule. Macro cultural moments stay in the brand's hands;
we build the engine underneath them.

A single post never pays for itself on code redemptions. At ₹2,000 per order it loses money twice over.
The same post pays for itself three ways, and only if the deal is structured for them:
1. Usage rights. The video runs as a whitelisted ad from the creator's handle for 6 months. Creator
   ads in this category routinely beat brand-shot ads on cost per purchase. That is where the fee is
   recovered.
2. The code lives for 90 days and the second and third posts from the same creator convert better
   than the first. One-and-done kills the return.
3. The 84-day story makes the content itself the product proof. A day-0 to day-84 video is reusable
   on the product page, in email, on Amazon A+ and in ads. A single "I love this serum" Reel is not.
So the plan below buys relationships, rights and a filmed timeline. It never buys posts.

LAYER 1: THE 84-DAY COHORT (PAID, HYBRID, FILMED)
What: twelve creators, eight micro (10k to 50k) and four mid (50k to 300k), use a named Arata routine
for 84 days and post four timeline videos at day 0, 28, 56 and 84, plus three Stories a month. Eight on
the hair fall routine (Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo ₹359 + Intensive Serum ₹571, or MitoActive ₹1,499 for the
four mid creators), two on the dandruff routine (S+C ₹866), two on anti-grey (₹449 serum, 3-month
plan).
Why: the 84-day claim is the brand's sharpest asset and nobody has filmed it. A day-0 video sets the
baseline honestly ("here is my crown today"). Day 28 is where the customer normally gives up; the
creator shows what "it is working" looks like at week 4. Day 84 closes the loop. Twelve creators gives

us statistical cover: some will see little change, and we let them say so. Honest mixed results read as
proof. Twelve identical raves read as ads.
Deal: hybrid. A flat fee that covers production for the 4-post package, plus ₹100 per first order on their

LAYER 2: NANO SEEDING AT VOLUME (PRODUCT ONLY)
What: 200 kits a month to nano creators (1k to 10k) matched to Dandruff Divya and Styling Sahil. Kit
one: Anti-Dandruff Shampoo 200 ml + Anti-Dandruff Serum 30 ml. Kit two: Hair Gel + Texturising
Spray. Each kit carries a personal code, a "Free gift" disclosure card and a Loox review link. No
obligation to post.
Why: styling combos are already the most-reviewed products on Loox, so this audience already posts
about Arata unasked. Dandruff's "95% in 1 wash" claim is the most filmable in the catalogue; the result
shows the same day. At a landed product cost of roughly ₹250 per kit plus ₹80 shipping (assumption),
200 kits cost about ₹66,000 a month. A 25% post rate (assumption, typical for well-targeted seeding)
gives 50 disclosed posts a month at ₹1,320 each, and the same creators feed reviews to the product
pages where Arata has 30 reviews on the Anti-Breakage Conditioner and none on the dandruff
conditioner.
Expected effect: 150 posts a quarter, a pipeline of proven nano creators to promote into Layer 1 and
the ambassador cohort, and review velocity on the under-reviewed SKUs.

LAYER 3: DERMATOLOGIST AND TRICHOLOGIST VOICES (PAID, CREDENTIALED)
What: four Instagram and YouTube dermatologists or trichologists, 20k to 300k followers, with
displayed qualifications. Each produces two Reels and one long-form explainer over 90 days on
MitoActive: what Y100 does, why mitochondrial function matters for the anagen to telogen ratio, how it
differs from Redensyl stacks and from minoxidil, and who it is for. Fee per post plus usage rights for
the product page and ads. No codes needed for the long-form; a vanity page arata.in/c/drname does
the tracking.
Why: ASCI's 2023 update means only qualified people can explain a mechanism. That restriction is a
gift to Arata, because no competitor in the ₹400 to ₹700 band has a mechanism worth explaining.
Clinical Kavya, the ₹1,499 buyer, needs a doctor to say "this is credible and it is not minoxidil". Derm
content also answers the brief's top objection, "Is this just another Redensyl serum?", from a mouth
the customer trusts.
Expected effect: eight short and four long derm videos, the trust layer the MitoActive page is missing,
and search-and-YouTube content that ranks for "minoxidil alternative" and "Y100 hair growth" for
years.

EXAMPLE 7: DERMATOLOGIST TALKING POINTS FOR MITOACTIVE (LAYER 3 ONLY)
The doctor decides what to say. These are the facts we can back, in the order a sceptical viewer
needs them.
1. Most hair growth serums at ₹550 to ₹1,100 are the same Redensyl, Anagain and Procapil stack at
   different percentages.
2. Minoxidil works but creates dependency, early shedding and side effects for some users, and most
   people start it without a doctor.
3. Y100 acts on mitochondrial function in the follicle. The ingredient has over six years of research
   and more than five clinical studies, including an Indian-participant study showing reduced
   shedding, improved anagen to telogen ratio and increased density.
4. MitoActive is a 30 ml, 1-month supply at twice-daily use, ₹1,499, roughly ₹50 a day. For
   comparison, a diagnosis-led kit runs ₹1,400 to ₹1,800 a month for five or more months.
5. It is positioned as a 3-month course plus maintenance, dermatologically tested, and it is not for
   advanced pattern baldness (Norwood 5 and above), which needs clinical intervention.
Point 5 is deliberate. A doctor who says who it is not for is the most convincing voice for who it is for.

DECISION RULES
Reviewed weekly, decided at day 45 and day 90.
If it works (blended cost per first order under ₹450 and day-84 completion at 10 of 12): scale Layer
1 to 36 creators in the next quarter with regional-language cohorts (Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali),
move the top four creators to a 12-month ambassador retainer, shift 20% of Meta prospecting budget
into whitelisted creator ads, and launch the ambassador cohort of 25 from graduates.
If one layer works and another does not: cut the weak layer, double the strong one. The most likely
pattern is Layer 2 seeding producing cheap posts but low orders, and Layer 1 producing orders. In that
case seeding stays at 200 kits a month for content and reviews, judged on cost per post, and the
order target moves fully to Layers 1 and 3.
If it does not work (cost per first order above ₹900 at day 90 across layers, or fewer than 6 of 12
creators complete): stop paying flat fees. Keep the content with the usage rights, run it as ads for the
remaining rights window, keep Layer 4 because it costs only product and MitoActive still needs
reviews, and rebuild the cohort as affiliate-only (commission, no flat fee) with creators who proved
they can finish 84 days.
A rule that overrides the others: a creator who misses disclosure twice or uses a banned claim once
is removed, rights kept, no second instalment. Compliance is the one metric with no tolerance.
