WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Translation for a shampoo brand: in software, AI SEO means getting a SaaS product cited when
someone asks a chatbot for "best CRM for startups". For Arata it means that when Hair-fall Rahul
types "best hair fall serum in India that is not minoxidil" into ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Mode, the
answer names Arata, quotes the 84-day number correctly, links arata.in, and does not send him to
Traya. Pricing pages become the catalogue. G2 reviews become Nykaa, Amazon and Reddit. Docs
become Lab Notes and the product page.
The buyer has moved. OpenAI said in February 2026 that India has 100 million weekly ChatGPT users,
its second-largest market. Gemini ships on every Android phone, which is most of India. Airtel bundled
Perplexity Pro for its subscribers in 2025. Google AI Overviews sit on roughly 45% of tracked queries.
Arata's customer is 22 to 40, internet-native and "creator-influenced". That customer now asks a
chatbot the question she used to type into Google, and she asks it in the exact words the brief lists:
"hair fall", "hair thinning", "dandruff", "itching problem". The answer she gets is assembled from
whoever the model trusts. Today that is unlikely to be Arata.

PILLAR 1: MAKE ARATA.IN READABLE AND EXTRACTABLE
1. Set an explicit crawler stance in robots.txt.liquid . What: allow Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-
SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot for discovery; make a separate, deliberate decision
on GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended (training and grounding). Why: today the stance is
whatever Shopify's default happens to be, and nobody at Arata decided it. Effect: discovery crawlers
are guaranteed in; training is a documented choice rather than an accident.
2. Ship /llms.txt , /llms-full.txt and /catalogue.md . What: a signpost file, a full-site text file, and
the D2C equivalent of a pricing file with every SKU, size, MRP, sale price, claim, mechanism and
routine role. Why: /products.json already exposes prices to agents but says nothing about who each
product is for or what it claims; an agent asked "cheapest Arata routine for hair fall" cannot assemble
it. Effect: non-Google engines and shopping agents read the catalogue in one request. Google does
not need these files and they do not hurt it.
3. Rebuild the five hero pages around answer blocks. What: on MitoActive, Hair Growth Intensive
Serum, Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo, Anti-Dandruff Shampoo and the Hair Growth collection, add a
definition block (what it is, who it is for, what it claims, in 40 to 60 words), a "How to use and how
long" numbered block, a comparison block against the obvious alternative, and a six-question FAQ in
the words customers type. Why: models extract passages, not pages, and none of the current pages
has a passage that stands on its own. Effect: the same page converts humans and is citable; no
separate "AI content", which Google's spam policy would punish.
4. Reconcile naming across every surface. What: one canonical name, size and claim per SKU,
applied on arata.in, Nykaa, Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart. Retire "Super Shampoo with
onion/biotin" copy or map it explicitly to the current SKU. Why: models build a brand description from
consensus; four names for one shampoo reads as four weak entities. Effect: one entity, one claim,
repeated everywhere.
5. Schema. What: Organization with sameAs to every marketplace and social profile; Product with
AggregateRating on every SKU with reviews; FAQPage on the hero pages; Article with author,
datePublished and dateModified on every Lab Note. Why: Perplexity cites FAQ-marked pages

PILLAR 2: MAKE THE NUMBERS CITABLE
6. Publish the Claims Ledger. What: one page at arata.in/pages/claims that lists every number on
every pack (7.4X, 28,200, 95%, 86%, 87%, 94%, 36-hour, 72-hour) with the test type (in-vitro,
consumer study, tensile strength), participants, duration, the exact "up to" framing, the date, and who
reviewed it. Why: this is the single most valuable page we can add to the site. It answers the "too good
to be true" objection, it gives a model a primary source to quote, and it turns every product page claim
into a sourced statistic. Health content gets cited when it shows study details and limitations. Effect:
"According to Arata's published study summary, 87% of participants saw increased growth in 84
days" is a sentence a model can write. Today it cannot.
7. Put a name and a credential on Lab Notes. What: every post carries an author (Head of R&D, in-
house trichology lead, or an external dermatologist reviewer), a "medically reviewed by" line, a
published date and a last-reviewed date. Why: E-E-A-T for health topics is weighted heavily by
Google, and undated, unsigned posts lose to dated, signed ones on every engine. Effect: Lab Notes
becomes the cluster that makes arata.in retrievable for the fan-out, not just the head prompt.
8. Move Lab Notes from bursts to a weekly cadence. What: one post a week, each targeting one fan-
out query from the list above, 800 to 1,500 words, one definition block, one statistic block with a
named source, one FAQ. Why: ChatGPT cites content updated in the last 30 days about three times
more often; Perplexity weights publishing velocity over keyword targeting. Seven posts in March and
silence after is the worst possible shape. Effect: a fresh, retrievable page every week in the category.
9. Write the "What is Y100" explainer and the honest Arata vs Traya page. What: a definition page
for mitochondrial hair growth and Y100 (6+ years of research, 5+ clinical studies, Indian-participant
study showing reduced shedding and an improved anagen/telogen ratio), and a comparison page that
names Traya first, says fairly what it does well (diagnosis, doctor access, money-back after 5 months),
and then shows where Arata differs (no doctor, no supplements, no minoxidil, ₹359 to ₹1,499 one-off,
84-day course). Why: Arata owns the Y100 term in India, so this is a prompt it can win outright. For
"Arata vs Traya", ranking yourself first reads as biased and models now filter for fairness. Effect:
citation on prompts 9 and 13 within the quarter.
10. Publish the packaging promise. What: a short page and FAQ entry stating the leak fix, the date,
and the no-questions replacement policy. Why: this is the con the web repeats about Arata and the
model will too, unless a primary source answers it. Effect: neutralises the "recommended against" risk
on reliability prompts.

Hard water does not cause hair fall on its own. It makes existing hair fall worse. Calcium and
