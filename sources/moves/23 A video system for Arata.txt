WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Translated to D2C: the product demo becomes the PDP and listing video, the explainer becomes the
claim explainer (how 7.4X was measured), the social clip becomes the Reel, and the ad stays the ad.
The method is the same. The surfaces change.
Arata's whole positioning is a number on the bottle: 7.4X, 28,200, 95%, 86%, 87%. A number on a
static pack image is a claim. A number shown being measured is proof. Right now the brand's proof
lives in text (Lab Notes, PDP copy) and the brand's entertainment lives in video (Samay Raina,
Taapsee). The two have not met. The proof has never been filmed.
That gap costs most on the two SKUs that matter most. MitoActive is ₹1,499 for 30 ml with zero
reviews and a mechanism (Y100, mitochondrial function) that nobody in the category has heard of. A
customer cannot read their way to ₹1,499. They need to see the mechanism, the pump, the 84-day
calendar and a real face. The Hair Growth Intensive Serum carries the "up to 28,200 new hair" claim
that the brief itself flags as the one customers find hard to believe. A 45-second video showing the
study design does what three paragraphs cannot.
The channel mix makes it worse. 68% of revenue sits on marketplaces and quick commerce.
Amazon.in gives a listing video the top slot in the image carousel and Nykaa shows video in the
gallery. Blinkit and Zepto give you a thumbnail and three lines, so video has to do its work before the
customer opens the app, on Reels and in the Meta feed. Every surface that Arata sells on either
rewards video directly or depends on video upstream.
The opportunity is that the competition is filmable but not provable. Pilgrim, Minimalist and Bare
Anatomy sell the same Redensyl and Anagain stack and shoot the same lifestyle footage. Arata has an
in-house lab in Delhi, a tensile strength test, a petri dish and a hair count grid. Nobody else at ₹359 to
₹571 can point a camera at that. Traya spends ₹138 Cr a year on marketing to tell a medical story.
Arata can tell a lab story for the cost of one shoot day and a template.

Signature moves, so any editor or template can reproduce the style:
1. The first two seconds are the problem, silent, no logo, no music. Hair on a pillow out-performs a
   bottle every time.
2. Every number gets its own card: 120px, centred, lab-blue on white, one second hold, a single ding.
3. Captions are word-by-word, bottom third, white with lab-blue highlight on the keyword (scalp,
   roots, 84 days).
4. A hard cut plus a 5% punch-in on every new sentence in the talking head. Nothing drifts.
5. The music dips for half a second before the honest caveat. The caveat is what makes the number
   believable.

The point of the structure: shoot once, template everything, render per surface. A new SKU or a

PHASE 1 (WEEK 1 TO 2): THE MASTER SHOOT AND THE PROOF LIBRARY
What: one 10-hour shoot day across the Delhi lab, a white studio and one outdoor location, plus six
real customers (two hair fall, two dandruff, one styling, one grey) recruited from Loox reviewers. Shot
list in the claim map above, plus texture (serum drop, shampoo lather, gel between fingers), pumps
and seals, and routine hands for each hero SKU.
Why: every asset downstream pulls from this library. Shooting the seal and the pump also answers the
brief's leak and broken-seal complaints on camera, in every PDP video, without saying a word about
leaks.
Effect: a library of roughly 120 usable clips that covers all five hero SKUs and four claims. The
MitoActive day-84 clock also starts here.

the library.
Why: the brand's own philosophy is recall through culture. Culture needs cadence. A burst in February
and silence in March loses the recall the burst bought.
Effect: 26 videos live by day 30, and a loop that keeps shipping without a new shoot.
