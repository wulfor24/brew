WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This is a D2C read of signup. Arata has no free trial and no software to register for. "Signup" here is
three moments: the point where a visitor gives you a phone number or email before they buy, the
identity and address step inside checkout, and the account that exists after the first order. Treat all
three as one flow and the job becomes clear: get the first order with as few fields as possible, and
leave behind a contact you are allowed to message when the 45-day reorder window opens.
The numbers make this worth the effort. arata.in gets about 650K visits a month and ships 50,000+
D2C orders a month. Own website is 32% of FY25 revenue, and the brief's stated primary conversion
is a first purchase of a hair fall or dandruff routine on arata.in, then a repeat inside 45 days. Every
field, every forced account, every OTP that fails on a slow Jio connection in Pune takes a slice of that.
Checkout abandonment on Indian D2C stores sits around 70% (industry benchmark, assumption). If
Arata's checkout holds at that level, roughly 1.2 lakh carts a month start and do not finish. Recovering
five percentage points of that is about 6,000 orders, close to ₹55 lakh a month at the implied ₹900 to
₹1,000 AOV.
The second cost is quieter. Arata has no subscription, no quiz, no community and no app. The only
way the brand can reach a first-time buyer on day 40 and say "your serum is about to run out" is a
phone number with WhatsApp consent, or an email with opt-in. The brief says beauty repeat rates sit
near 26% and routine-led models pass 40%. A shopper who checks out as a guest and never opts in
is a one-time customer by design. The opt-in is the bridge between a ₹571 serum sale and the 84-day
routine the product claims need to prove themselves.
The third cost is trust at the exact wrong moment. The top negative review theme is "the seal was
broken" and "the shampoo bottle was leaking". The brief lists the shopper's anxieties as "Will I waste
another ₹600?", "Is 30 ml enough?" and "Will the bottle leak?". Those questions arrive at checkout,
and today checkout does not answer them. A ₹1,499 MitoActive buyer with zero reviews to lean on is
deciding with a payment screen in front of her and nothing on it that says "if it leaks we replace it".

LAYER 1: VALUE BEFORE THE ASK
The skill's second principle is to show value before asking for commitment. For Arata that means the
opt-in popup stops being a coupon box and becomes the first step of the routine. A three-question
picker (what is bothering you, how long, hair type) returns a named routine with a price: "Hair fall
starter: Anti-Hair Fall Shampoo ₹359 + Growth Serum ₹571". The WhatsApp number is asked after the
result, framed as "send me this routine and my 84-day plan". The shopper gets something they
wanted before giving anything.
Expected effect: a phone-led capture with a reason runs well above an email coupon popup. We target
a 6% capture rate on cold traffic against the 1 to 3% benchmark for coupon popups (assumption).
Cart thresholds are the second value-first fix. The ₹299 free shipping and ₹999 free gift thresholds
already exist. A progress bar in the cart drawer ("Add ₹69 for your free gift") moves ₹930 carts to
₹999+ and lifts AOV without a new offer.

LAYER 2: PHONE-FIRST IDENTITY
Checkout opens with one field: a 10-digit phone number, tel keyboard, +91 fixed. OTP arrives by SMS
with WhatsApp as the fallback channel, and auto-reads on Android. Under the OTP field sits a single
pre-ticked consent line for WhatsApp order updates and routine messages. The consent is specific,
revocable in one reply, and the first message confirms it. That is the Meta business messaging
requirement and it is also what makes the day-40 nudge possible.
If the number is known to the checkout network, name, address and last payment method prefill. The
shopper is three taps from done. If the number is new, the address block is pincode first (which fills
city and state and checks serviceability), then name, address line 1 with autocomplete, landmark
optional.

Why a checkout layer rather than stock Shopify: Shop Pay is not available to Indian stores, so the
prefill that Shopify stores elsewhere get for free has to come from an Indian network. GoKwik reports
165M+ shoppers on its network and a 10% conversion uplift with Honasa. Shiprocket Checkout is the
alternative. We do not pick for Arata; we run the install on whichever Arata's ops team already has a
relationship with and measure.
Expected effect: a 4 to 6 point lift in checkout completion from prefill and field reduction combined
(assumption, based on the published partner numbers and the field count going from 12 to 4).

LAYER 3: THE ACCOUNT BUILDS ITSELF
Shopify's new customer accounts log in with a one-time code sent to email or phone. There is no
password anywhere in the system. Every order creates the account; the thank-you page says so and
the first email carries a login link. The shopper never fills a registration form, and the account still
exists for order tracking, reorder and, when it launches, subscription management.
Expected effect: account creation rate moves from whatever the password form gets today
(benchmark 10 to 20% of orders, assumption) to 100% of orders, with the login step deferred to the
first time it is useful.

DECISION RULES
If checkout completion lifts 3 points or more by day 45: keep the layer at 100%, move the prepaid
incentive test live, and extend prefill to the quick-commerce-style "reorder in one tap" page. Start the
subscription work on top of the account, since every shopper now has one.
If checkout completion lifts under 3 points: hold the quick wins (they cost nothing), split the data by
step. If the phone step is losing people, test OTP via WhatsApp first. If the address step is losing
people, check the pincode serviceability copy and the autocomplete quality. If payment is losing
people, the trust lines and COD rules are the suspects. Re-read at day 75.
If WhatsApp opt-in is under 40% in the pre-ticked arm: the consent copy is reading as spam.
Rewrite with the cadence stated inside the consent line itself and test again.
If STOP rate passes 5%: the day-7 or day-21 message is over-selling. Cut to order updates plus day
40 only.
If day-40 reorder rate on opted-in orders passes 35% by day 90: the opt-in is worth more than the
order. Move the picker to the homepage hero and make the WhatsApp send the primary CTA on the
result screen, ahead of add to cart.
