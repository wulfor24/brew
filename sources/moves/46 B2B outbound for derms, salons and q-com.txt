WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Cold email in the software world means writing to a stranger at a company and asking for a meeting.
For Arata the equivalent is B2B outbound: writing to the dermatologist, the salon owner, the HR head
and the quick-commerce category manager, and asking each of them for one small thing that moves
product. Same craft, different buyers.
Arata already moves about 2 lakh B2B units a month. That number is almost entirely marketplace and
quick-commerce purchase orders. There is no outbound function behind it (assumption: we found no
partnerships or trade role in the public team, and no "for professionals" or "for clinics" page on
arata.in). The B2B channel grows when a platform decides to reorder. Nobody at Arata is making the
first move.
That matters for three reasons. First, 68% of revenue sits on rented land: marketplaces 38%, quick
commerce 30%. The people who decide where a hair fall shampoo ranks in a Blinkit search or
whether MitoActive gets onboarded in Hyderabad are a few dozen category managers. Nobody is
writing to them with a point of view. Second, MitoActive launched in August 2026 at ₹1,499 with zero
reviews and a claim set (87% saw growth in 84 days, 5+ clinical studies on Y100) that is built for a
dermatologist to repeat. Clinical Kavya, the persona who will pay ₹1,499 without blinking, trusts her
derm more than any ad. Third, the brand line is "systems, not symptoms", and the places where a
routine gets recommended in person, the salon chair and the clinic, are exactly where Arata has no
presence.
The cost of leaving this alone is quiet. MitoActive stays a D2C-only product fighting Traya on Meta,
where Traya spends ₹138 Cr a year. Salons keep recommending L'Oréal Professionnel backbar.
Category managers keep treating Arata as one more Redensyl brand with a slightly better price.
The opportunity is cheap. A full outbound setup costs less per month than one week of a mid-size
Meta campaign, and the assets it creates (derm recommendations, salon shelf presence, category
manager relationships, corporate bulk orders) compound. This document gives Arata the sending

WHAT EACH BUYER IS DEALING WITH TODAY
Dermatologists and trichologists. The brief says 5M urban Indians are on medicated OTC products
and 1M on clinical treatment. Their doctors have two tools for hair fall: minoxidil 5% (Mintop, ₹600 to
₹900, dependency and shedding) and finasteride. Patients ask for something that is not minoxidil
every day. The doctor has no good answer at the ₹600 to ₹1,500 level that comes with study data.
MitoActive's Y100 story (mitochondrial function, anagen/telogen ratio, an Indian-participant study
showing reduced shedding and increased density) is the first thing in this price band a derm can
explain to a patient in two sentences. The objection will be "show me the study", and the answer must
be the study, not a landing page.
Quick-commerce category managers. 30% of Arata's revenue runs through Blinkit, Zepto and
Instamart. The day-to-day contact is an account manager who handles purchase orders and fill rates.
Above that person sits a category head for personal care who decides which brands get a hero tile in
"hair fall", which get into new cities, and which get a slot in the monsoon dandruff push. These people
get pitched by every D2C brand on the platform. What they do not get is a brand telling them
something true about their own category. Arata can: it has 2,248 on-site reviews, 650K monthly visits

Rules that hold all four together: a reply on any channel stops every channel. An opt-out on one is an

COPY RULES WE HOLD OURSELVES TO
    Under 80 words in email 1. No link in email 1. At most one link later, never shortened.
    Subject lines of 2 to 4 words, lowercase, no numbers, no product names. They should look like a
    colleague wrote them.
    "You" and "your" outnumber "I" and "we". The first line is about their patient, their shelf, their
    category, their employees.
    Every claim traces to the brief: 7.4X, 95% fungus reduction in 1 wash, 87% saw growth in 84 days,
    up to 28,200 new hairs in 84 days, 5+ clinical studies on Y100. Nothing else gets said.
    Plain text, no images, no attachments. Opt-out line and postal address in every signature.
    Open and click tracking off. We measure replies.

WHY THESE PLAYS AND NOT OTHERS

thousand sends look like bulk mail, and the four buyers do not share a problem. We also ruled out

Decision rules at day 90.
If a segment hits its reply and outcome targets, double its daily volume (more mailboxes on the same
domains, never more sends per mailbox), hand the proven copy to a second sender, and add the next
city.
If derms work and q-com does not, the derm clinic codes become the review engine for MitoActive
and we take the "derm-recommended" line into every other discipline. If q-com works and derms do
not, we shift the derm budget into the salon play, which shares the same counter-card asset.
If a segment misses on reply rate but the list is clean (bounces under 1%, placement above 90%), the
copy or the offer is wrong. We rewrite email 1 with a different first line and the follow-ups with a
different proof point, and run another 300 before deciding.
If a segment misses on outcome but not on replies (people answer, nobody buys), the offer is the
problem. For salons that usually means margin; for derms it means the kit asked for too much from the
clinic; for corporate it means the session needs a derm speaker, not a brand person.
If bounces or complaints breach the stop-loss on any domain, that domain comes out of rotation, the
spare goes in, and the program does not stop.
