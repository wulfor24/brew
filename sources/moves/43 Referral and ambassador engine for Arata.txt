WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
For a shampoo and serum brand, "referrals" means three things: a refer-a-friend loop for customers,
an affiliate tier for creators and coupon partners, and an ambassador cohort that documents results in
public. We treat reviews as the fourth leg, because a review is a referral to a stranger.
Arata serves 1.5 million customers a year and ships 50,000 D2C orders a month. Not one of those
customers has a way to bring in a friend. A customer who writes "Hair fall reduced drastically" on
Loox gets a thank-you and nothing else. There is no referral link, no credit wallet, no pre-written
WhatsApp message, no reason to forward. The brand built its proof into the pack (7.4X, 28,200, 87%)
and then left the most believable proof, a friend saying "it worked for me", on the table.
This category spreads by mouth more than most. Hair fall is first noticed by a partner or a parent. The
job the customer hires Arata for is "show me it is working within 2 to 3 months". A sceptic who has
burned ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 on three to five failed products does not believe a claim. They believe their
flatmate. Referral is the mechanism that turns a satisfied Rahul in Bangalore into a first order from his
colleague.
The cost of not having one shows up in two of the brief's known gaps. First, 68% of revenue sits on
marketplaces and quick commerce. A referral only tracks on arata.in, so every referred order is an
order pulled onto owned land, with the customer's phone number and email attached. Second, there is
no subscription. Referral credit that expires in 90 days is the cheapest repeat trigger available, and it
lands squarely inside the 45-day reorder window Arata already targets.
Competitors are moving. Traya runs affiliate programs through networks like Cuelinks, and in April
2026 ran #ProofHaiBoss, pairing creators one-to-one with verified customers to film transformation
Reels for a ₹25 lakh prize pool. Arata has better numbers on the pack, a younger brand, and the
Samay Raina cultural capital. It also has MitoActive, a ₹1,499 serum with zero reviews two months
after launch. Nobody forwards a ₹1,499 product with no reviews. The engine below fixes both.

LAYER 2: REVIEWS (PRIORITY 2, WEEKS 1 TO 3)
What: a timed, neutral review request per SKU, sent to every buyer who reaches the milestone,
whatever their sentiment. Shampoo and conditioner at day 30. Serums, including MitoActive, at day

Why: MitoActive is Arata's flagship and has zero reviews. The referral loop cannot carry a product that

Complaints (leaks, broken seals) go to a service recovery flow that runs separately from the review
ask.
Expected effect: MitoActive at 100+ Loox reviews within 90 days of the first day-84 cohort. On a
50,000-order base, a 3% review rate on delivered invitations produces far more than that; the
constraint is only the 84-day lag.

LAYER 3: CREATOR AFFILIATES (PRIORITY 3, WEEKS 3 TO 6)

cookie. 15-day hold for returns, monthly UPI payout above ₹1,000.
Why the power law matters: around 20% of affiliates will produce 80% of orders. We will recruit 100
and then spend the relationship effort on 20. Those 20 get MitoActive early, custom landing pages
("Priya's hair fall routine") and a direct WhatsApp line to the Brew partnerships lead.
Channel policy written before day one: no bidding on "Arata" or "MitoActive" keywords, no posting
codes to coupon aggregators, no claims beyond the pack (the 28,200 number is "up to", in 84 days;
creators say that or nothing).
Expected effect: 3% of new arata.in customers from affiliates by day 90, growing to 6% to 8% by
month 6 as the super-promoters compound.
