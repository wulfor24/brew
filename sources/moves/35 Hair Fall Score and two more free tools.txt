WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This is the D2C version of engineering as marketing. Where a software company would ship a website
grader or an ROI calculator, Arata ships a hair fall score, a hair fall calculator and a routine builder. Same
logic: give away something useful, let it do the diagnosis, and let the product be the obvious next step.
The brief lists two gaps that this discipline closes in one move: Arata has no quiz and no regimen builder.
Today a visitor with hair fall lands on the Hair Growth collection and sees a ₹1,499 serum, a ₹571 serum, a
₹999 pack of 3 and a ₹359 shampoo, each with its own number, and has to work out the answer alone.
Traya built a ₹343 Cr business on the opposite experience: a free hair test first, a kit second. Bare Anatomy
runs a quiz. Arata, the brand with a number on every bottle, has no tool that puts a number on the customer.
That matters because the customer's job is "stop the hair fall I see on the pillow and in the drain, and show
me it is working within 2 to 3 months". The first half of that sentence is a measurement problem. Every
persona in the brief starts with a question they cannot answer: Rahul does not know if crown thinning is
normal, Ritika does not know if post-partum shedding will stop on its own, Kavya wants something a derm
would respect but cannot tell where she sits on the scale. The brand's core promise is zero guesswork. The
site currently asks for a lot of guessing.
The economics are simple. Arata gets about 650K visits a month and converts roughly 7 to 8% of visits to
orders including repeat buyers. A tool that even a fifth of new visitors touch, and that captures a WhatsApp
number from a third of those, builds a first-party list of tens of thousands of named, scored, segmented hair
fall prospects every month. Right now 68% of revenue sits on marketplaces and quick commerce where
Arata does not own the customer. A score with a phone number attached is the cheapest way we know to
move people onto land Arata owns.
The tools also solve two other listed problems as a side effect. MitoActive has zero reviews; a score taken at
day 0 and again at day 84 is the most natural review request a brand can send. And the collection page
mixes claims with no routine; the routine builder gives every score band one named cart with one price,
which is what Routine Ritika asked for.

THE PLAN
PHASE 1 (DAYS 1 TO 30): THE HAIR FALL SCORE WITH THE ROUTINE BUILDER INSIDE IT
What: a 10-question, 60-second assessment at arata.in/score. Output is a score from 0 to 100, one of four
bands, a plain-language reason, and one recommended routine with one price and one button. The full 84-
day plan and a saved score card are released against a WhatsApp number or email.
Why: it is the analyzer pattern, the closest match to the one case in this discipline that produced hundreds
of thousands of leads with near-total auto-qualification. The questions double as segmentation. We learn
trigger (post-partum, illness, stress, new city), scalp state (flaky, oily, dry), history (tried onion, biotin,
minoxidil, a derm) and family pattern before the customer has spent a rupee. That data decides which
routine we show and which messages follow.
Expected effect: a named, scored list that grows every day from existing traffic; a higher add-to-cart rate
from the Hair Growth collection because the choice is made for the visitor; a day-0 baseline that makes the
day-84 review request natural.
Gating decision: partially gated. The score and the band are shown to everyone, instantly. The full plan, the
saved score card and the day-84 reminder need a phone number or email. Reason: a fully gated tool kills
usage and share, a fully ungated one leaves no list. Showing the number first and asking for the phone
second is the honest order.

PHASE 1 (DAYS 15 TO 30): THE HAIR FALL CALCULATOR
What: a single page at arata.in/hair-fall-calculator. Four inputs (strands on the pillow, strands on the comb,
strands in the shower, washes per week). Output: estimated daily shed, where it sits against the 50 to 100
strands a day that dermatologists treat as normal, and the 84-day picture. Ungated. One link to the score.
Why: "how much hair fall is normal" is the largest single question in the category and nobody in India
answers it with a tool. Calculators skew to reach and share. A shareable result card ("I lose about 140
strands a day") is the kind of thing that travels on Instagram and gets quoted in articles, which is where the

    Day-84 re-score: the same 10 questions, sent automatically 84 days after the first score. The result page

BUILD DECISION
Build custom, inside the Shopify theme as a self-contained page with a small scoring script. Reasons: the
routine output has to add real products to the real cart, the question logic is Arata's own, and the design has
to carry the 3.0 lab-blue identity. A no-code quiz tool would get a version live in three days, but it would live
on a subdomain, it would not write to the cart, and the lead data would sit in a third party. The scoring
engine is a lookup table, so the custom build is two weeks of one front-end developer plus our creative
lead.
What we leave out of the MVP, by the rule: accounts, photo upload, saved results beyond the WhatsApp
card, a PDF report, and a "compare with friends" feature. If the day-30 numbers justify it, the day-84 re-
score comes next.

5. How long before a hair fall routine shows results? (a full hair cycle is about 84 days; judge nothing before

6. When should I see a dermatologist? (patches, sudden loss, scalp pain, or hair fall with other symptoms)

PHASE-2 BRIEF: HARD WATER CHECKER
    URL: arata.in/hard-water. Input: city, or pincode. Output: a hardness read (soft, moderate, hard, very
    hard), what that does to hair (mineral build-up, dull strands, scalp irritation, more breakage), and a
    routine adjustment (clarifying wash frequency, conditioner on the lengths, the Anti-Hair Fall shampoo's
    hard-water framing).
    Data: a city-level table built from public groundwater and municipal water quality reports for the top 50
    Indian cities, labelled with the source and year on the page. Pincode-level is phase 3 if the city version
    earns links.
    Why phase 2: data sourcing and a maintenance commitment (annual refresh). The share-worthiness is
    high ("Bangalore water is this hard?") and so is the link potential from city publications.
    Gating: none. Link to the score at the bottom.

    5 questions on the same engine: flake size and colour, itch timing, oiliness, response to washing more
    often, season.
    Output: dandruff (fungal, oily) → Anti-Dandruff Shampoo + Conditioner ₹866 or shampoo alone ₹404;
    dry scalp → Damage Defence Super Shampoo ₹395 and wash less; both → add the Anti-Dandruff Serum
    ₹299.
    Target query cluster: "dandruff or dry scalp", "dandruff vs dry scalp", "why does my scalp itch after
    washing".
