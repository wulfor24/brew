WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
For a D2C haircare brand, "events" means something different from a SaaS trade show. Here it means
derm conferences, college fests, salon activations, mall and office pop-ups, creator meetups and live
sessions. The buyer is an individual spending ₹359 to ₹1,499, so we judge every event on scalp
checks, first orders, reviews and derm recommendations, never on footfall.
Arata sells a number on every bottle. 7.4X, 28,200, 95%, 87%. The customer wants to believe it and
has been burned before. The brief says it plainly: "Will I waste another ₹600?" is the anxiety, and
scepticism from claims is the emotional tension. Digital can repeat a claim. It cannot let someone see
their own crown on a screen, feel the Hair Gel on their fingers, or hear a dermatologist say the
mechanism is real. That is the job of in-person. Arata has no physical retail at scale, no app and no
community, so right now there is no place where a sceptical buyer meets the brand in person.
Two things make this urgent. MitoActive launched in August 2026 at ₹1,499 and has zero reviews. A
₹1,499 serum with a mitochondrial story needs dermatologists and early users vouching for it before
paid media can carry it. Second, 68% of revenue sits on marketplaces and quick commerce, where
Arata is one Redensyl-adjacent tile among Pilgrim, Minimalist and Bare Anatomy. Events are the one
channel where nobody else is on the shelf next to you.
The founders already think this way. Dhruv Madhok's stated philosophy is cultural fit over category fit,
and brand recall as the win. The Samay Raina mock podcast was that idea in media. Events are that
idea in the real world: the brand shows up where 22 to 40 year olds already are, and gives them
something useful before asking for anything.
The cost of doing nothing is specific. Every month without an event programme is a month with no
first-party before-and-after photos, no derm sampling data for MitoActive, no on-camera testimonials,
and no in-person answer to "is this just another Redensyl serum?" The opportunity is a small,
repeatable portfolio that costs less than one week of marketplace ads and produces assets paid media
cannot.

an option, because people who got the check and bought on Blinkit a week later will never use the
code. We count influence inside a 90-day window and never claim last-click credit for an order the
event only touched.
Decision rules.
    Pop-ups: judged on a three-event trend, never on the first. Cost per first order under 2x paid CAC
    by event three → add Bangalore and go to four weekends a month in Delhi. Between 2x and 3x but
    event-cohort repeat beats digital by 10 points → keep going, the lifetime math works. Over 3x with
    flat repeat → cut the mall format first and keep only free-venue offices and gyms.
    DERMACON: judged on sampling sign-ups and redeemed patient codes within 90 days, never on
    conference-week orders. 40 derms and 60 redemptions → a standing derm programme and the
    2028 dinner. Under 15 derms → the dinner format is wrong for this audience; try a CME-hosted talk
    instead.
