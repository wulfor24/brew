WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
Arata is the only brand in the ₹400 to ₹700 hair fall band that puts a study number on every bottle.
7.4X. 28,200. 95%. 86%. 87%. That is a creative asset most D2C haircare brands would pay a lot to
own. Today it lives on the pack and on the product page. It does not yet run the ad account.
What we see in the brand's paid creative is a split personality. The culture work is strong: the Samay
Raina mock podcast was built for recall, and the founders say so plainly. But recall ads do not sell a
₹571 serum to a 28-year-old in Bangalore who has already burned ₹3,000 on onion shampoo and
biotin gummies. He needs a number, a timeline and a reason to believe. The brief tells us he is
"sceptical from being burned by claims". The ad has to do the convincing before the landing page
gets a chance.
The gap costs Arata in three places. First, MitoActive launched in August at ₹1,499 with zero reviews.
A product with a new mechanism (mitochondrial function, Y100) needs education before it sells, and
nothing in the current creative mix teaches it. Second, 68% of revenue sits on marketplaces and quick
commerce, where Arata pays rent on every order. The ad account is the main lever to grow the 32%
that runs through arata.in. Third, with ~650K monthly visits growing 42% month on month, creative
fatigue is the next wall. A brand growing that fast burns through audiences and needs a creative
pipeline, with a retro loop, rather than a campaign at a time.
The opportunity is simple to state. Arata has 2,248 on-site reviews and 2,435 Nykaa ratings full of
verbatim customer language, six claims that are defensible, and two founders with a real origin story.

COMPLIANCE GUARDRAILS FOR HAIR CLAIMS
Hair growth is a regulated claim space in India and a restricted one on Meta. Three rules apply to
every concept in this document.
1. Every number carries its qualifier. "Up to 28,200 new hairs within 84 days" always includes "up to"
   and "84 days". "87% saw increased growth" includes "in 84 days". The words "regrow", "cure" and
   "100%" do not appear anywhere.
2. No before/after scalp photos in paid. Meta's personal health and appearance policy restricts
   imagery that implies unexpected results or triggers negative self-perception. We use text-based
   progression ("day 1 → day 84"), bottle counts and pillow shots. Crown close-ups and bald-patch
   zooms stay out.
3. Advertising standards complaints against hair growth ads are upheld often. We keep the study
   design one tap away (Lab Notes explainer) and mirror the pack language exactly, so the ad never
   claims more than the bottle does.

Decision rules
    No verdict before 1,000 impressions per concept. A two-day read is a coin flip.
    Judge concepts, not ads. Three executions of "84 days, one number" failing means the concept is
    wrong. One failing means the execution was.
    A full-funnel loser with one strong metric is a lead. It goes back to the icebox as an iteration, with
    the weak stage named.
    If Lane A winners hold for 30 days: shift to 60% winner-led variations, keep 15% exploration alive,
    raise Lane A budget share by 10 points.
    If Lane B shows no single-metric hit in 30 days: the VSL mechanism framing is wrong, so we test
    the price-math hook and the upgrade-from-Intensive-Serum hook before touching production
    budget again.
    If rolling reach drops two weeks in a row: deploy the next creator partnership ad before adding any
    more statics.
    If CTR is strong and CVR is weak on any concept: fix the landing page before iterating the ad.
