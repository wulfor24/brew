WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
One line of translation first. In software, a co-marketing partner is a tool your customer already uses
before or after yours. For Arata, it is a brand that already sits in the same bathroom, the same cart, or
the same stressful month as the hair fall. The shower filter on the tap. The protein the customer drinks.
The shirt they bought for the wedding. The PCOS clinic they just joined. The co-living room they
moved into last week. Each of those brands owns a moment that ends with someone staring at hair in
the drain.
Today Arata buys its reach. The Samay Raina mock podcast and the Taapsee "CurlFriend" campaign
were the right instinct (borrow a culture the audience already loves) but both were paid celebrity work
with a start and a stop. Brand-to-brand partnerships give the same borrowed-audience effect at close
to zero media cost, and they do not expire when the campaign ends. As far as the brief and the live
site show, Arata has no co-branded bundle, no partner landing page and no audience swap running.
Assumption: that is the baseline.
The cost of leaving this empty is visible in the channel mix. 68% of FY25 revenue came through
marketplaces and quick commerce, where Blinkit, Zepto and Nykaa own the customer and set the
discount. Partner traffic lands on arata.in, where Arata keeps the margin, the email, the WhatsApp
number and the review. Every partner campaign we design below ends on an Arata-owned page.
The opportunity is in the triggers the brief already lists. Hair fall panic is set off by hard water, a new
city, post-partum shedding, stress and a humid commute. Dandruff flares in monsoon and winter.
Styling panic peaks before weddings. None of those triggers are owned by a haircare brand. They are
owned by a water brand, a co-living brand, a mom brand, a fashion brand and a health brand. If Arata
shows up inside those moments with the number on the bottle (7.4X, 28,200, 87%), it reaches the
customer before she searches "hair fall shampoo" and before Pilgrim or Traya can bid on her.
There is a second payoff. Arata's AOV sits around ₹900 to ₹1,000, with no subscription and no routine
builder. A co-branded kit is a routine with a reason. The Hard Water Kit we build in the worked
examples carries a ₹2,000-plus ticket, and it gives MitoActive a clinical doorway (a PCOS clinic's

Three findings fall out of this map.
1. The strongest partners are upstream of the problem. A shower filter brand and a co-living brand
   both meet the customer on the day the hard water starts. Arata's "made for Indian hard water,
   humidity and heat" claim is the bridge, and no competitor in the ₹400 to ₹700 band has a hard
   water story to match.
2. The "honest number" brands are the best brand-alignment partners. Arata puts a study number on
   every bottle. The Whole Truth Foods puts the full ingredient list on the front of the pack. The
   audiences overlap (urban, 24 to 38, label readers, burned by claims) and the message writes itself:
   two brands that show their work.
3. Several obvious names are off the table. Beardo and Bombay Shaving Company carry hair growth
   SKUs. The Moms Co belongs to Honasa, which owns Mamaearth. Clinikally and SkinKraft sell their
   own hair serums. Partnering with any of them hands the customer a competing bottle.

LAYER 0: PERMISSIONLESS CO-MARKETING (NO CONTRACT, WEEK 1 TO 2)
What: Build pages and posts around the brands, cities and habits the audience already lives in, without
asking anyone. The move that made Notion famous was publishing templates for Airbnb and Tesla;
Arata's version is publishing the hair fall answer for Bangalore borewell water, for the Cult.fit evening
slot, for the Rapido commute and for month four after delivery.
Why: These pages capture search intent that no competitor is writing for ("Bangalore water hair fall",
"helmet dandruff", "post-partum hair fall when does it stop"), they cost a writer and a designer, and
they test which trigger pulls hardest before any partner meeting.
Expected effect: 8 to 12 indexed pages inside 30 days, each with the relevant Arata SKU and a single
CTA. Assumption on traffic: 3,000 to 8,000 organic visits a month across the set by day 90.
Rules: name a city, a habit or a product category freely. Name a brand only in editorial copy ("if you
ride a Rapido daily") and never on packaging, in ads or in a way that implies endorsement.

LAYER 1: AUDIENCE SWAPS AND JOINT GIVEAWAYS (WEEK 2 TO 5)
What: Three low-friction formats with the two partners who have the biggest engaged audiences (The
Whole Truth Foods, Snitch) and one with the easiest yes (WaterScience).

STRUCTURING EACH DEAL
Every partnership gets the same one-page agreement (worked example 5) covering lead ownership,
promotion minimums, who builds what, timeline, branding approval, costs and the metrics both sides
share after. The rule that keeps these deals clean: Arata owns every lead captured on arata.in, the
partner owns every lead on their domain, and the giveaway form on arata.in captures consent for
both.
