WHY THIS MATTERS FOR ARATA
This is a D2C read of experimentation. The "signup" is an add to cart, the "plan selection" is a first
order of a hair fall or dandruff routine, and the "retention" metric is a reorder inside 45 days. The test
surfaces are arata.in product pages, the collection page, the cart, checkout, and the WhatsApp and
email messages that follow an order.
Arata is in the middle of three big changes at once: a full rebrand to "Hair & Scalp Science Co.", a new
₹1,499 hero in MitoActive with zero reviews, and traffic growing about 42% month on month to
roughly 650K visits. Each change touches the pages that produce 50,000+ D2C orders a month. As
far as we can tell from the outside, none of it is being split-tested (assumption). Every rebrand we
have seen ship without a holdout has cost the brand a conversion point it did not notice for a quarter.
The cost of guessing is large and invisible. The website is 32% of ₹61.1 Cr FY25 revenue, so roughly
₹19.5 Cr, and closer to ₹24 Cr on the ₹75 Cr FY26 forecast. A rebrand that quietly drops conversion by
5% costs ₹1.2 Cr a year and shows up in the dashboard as "traffic mix". A price block on MitoActive
that reads as expensive costs the hero its launch. You find these by running old and new side by side
and counting.
The opportunity is that Arata has enough traffic to decide most questions in two weeks. 650K visits a
month is about 21,700 sessions a day. A cart test reaches its sample in under a week, a product page
test in eight to fourteen days, and only MitoActive, as a new page, needs four weeks. Arata can run
four to six tests a month without the tests fighting for traffic.
The plan below instruments the funnel so there is a baseline, builds a backlog from the gaps already in
the brief (no routine builder, no cost-per-day framing, no leak-proof promise, no reorder nudge, no
subscription), scores it, and ships twelve tests in 90 days. Each has a written hypothesis, a pre-
committed sample size and a decision rule, so winners go into a playbook and losers teach us
something before the next one.

RUNNING RULES
1. One test per surface at a time. A cart test and a PDP test can overlap; two tests on the same page
   cannot.
2. 50/50 split, assigned once per visitor in a first-party cookie plus the customer ID when logged in.
   Price and offer tests (H7, H9, H15, H17) are assigned by customer and rendered server-side so no
   one sees two prices.
3. Minimum run is 14 days and must include the 1st to 3rd of a month, because salaried buyers cluster
   there and weekends spike. A test that reaches sample on day 3 still runs to day 14.
4. Maximum run is 28 days for a page test and 90 days for a lifecycle read. Not at sample by day 28:
   stop and report the interval.
5. No peeking. The weekly review looks at guardrails and data quality only. A guardrail breach stops
   the test; a good-looking primary metric does not.
6. No changes and no new traffic sources mid-test. If the rebrand must touch a page under test, the
   test restarts. A Samay Raina-style spike or a new Meta campaign landing on the serum page gets
   logged and read as its own segment.
7. Sale periods (Big Billion Days, Nykaa Pink Friday, Diwali) are blackout windows.

WHAT HALF A POINT IS WORTH
At 650K visits and ₹900 to ₹1,000 AOV, half a point of blended conversion is about 3,250 orders and
roughly ₹30 lakh a month. A 15% relative lift on a 22% repeat rate across 50,000 orders a month is
about 1,650 extra second orders, close to ₹15 lakh a month, and those are the customers who go on to
buy the routine.
